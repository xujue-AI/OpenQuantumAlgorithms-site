Known-input amplification of a thermal preparation

The source circuit prepares a Bell purification from all-zero wires and applies an explicit Pauli LCU approximation to an imaginary-time filter. This notebook amplifies the known prepared input by reflecting about all input wires. It keeps the accepted purification’s direction, including a possible global sign, while the thermal-polynomial bias remains unchanged.

The fixed model is H/E0=0.6Z+0.4XH/E_0=0.6Z+0.4XH/E0​=0.6Z+0.4X, E0=kB=1E_0=k_B=1E0​=kB​=1, Taylor degree 6 and default β=1.25/E0\beta=1.25/E_0β=1.25/E0​. One system, one reference and two label qubits give four wires. The primary amplitude-amplification identity is due to Brassard et al. (2000 preprint, published 2002); this implementation and its circuit figures are original.

Reflections and the actual access¶

The success reflection Sf=I−2Πlabels=0S_f=I-2\Pi_{\mathrm{labels}=0}Sf​=I−2Πlabels=0​ acts as identity on system and reference. The initial reflection S0=I−2∣0all⟩⟨0all∣S_0=I-2|0_{\mathrm{all}}\rangle\langle0_{\mathrm{all}}|S0​=I−2∣0all​⟩⟨0all​∣ acts on the whole input. Both are X–MCZ–X circuits. Starting with AAA, each chronological round is Sf,A†,S0,AS_f,A^\dagger,S_0,ASf​,A†,S0​,A, with the required global π\piπ phase, so Q=−AS0A†SfQ=-AS_0A^\dagger S_fQ=−AS0​A†Sf​.

The boxes below have actual circuit definitions: AAA contains Bell preparation and PREPARE/SELECT/unprepare. Resource counting uses the fully expanded circuits, and all reference wires stay coherent until amplification finishes.

diagram = QuantumCircuit(source.num_qubits)

Signed direction and the reduced thermal state¶

For p=∥ΠfA∣0⟩∥2p=\|\Pi_f A|0\rangle\|^2p=∥Πf​A∣0⟩∥2 and θ=arcsin⁡p\theta=\arcsin\sqrt pθ=arcsinp​, the accepted branch is multiplied by sin⁡[(2r+1)θ]/p\sin[(2r+1)\theta]/\sqrt psin[(2r+1)θ]/p​. A negative factor changes only its global sign. We check the raw signed branch first, then phase-invariant purification error and the reduced density matrix. The Gibbs trace-distance bias comes from the source polynomial and cannot be removed by this amplification.

Complete attempts and separate energy groups¶

Every complete attempt resets all wires, runs the whole amplified unitary, measures the labels, resets the reference without conditioning, changes the system basis and reads the system. Failed attempts run this same schedule and are charged in full. The noncommuting energy mean is 0.6⟨Z⟩+0.4⟨X⟩0.6\langle Z\rangle+0.4\langle X\rangle0.6⟨Z⟩+0.4⟨X⟩, so X and Z need separate accepted samples.

The tables are logical counts and unexecuted workload proposals. Coherent rotation synthesis, physical gate/reset/readout errors, scheduling and error correction remain unavailable. Hypothetical synthesis allowances cover every AAA, inverse and reflection call; irreversible reset/readout faults are separate.

Optional local exploration and scope¶

Integer rounds are not monotonic. At β=2\beta=2β=2, three rounds bring success near 0.9925 and a fourth reduces it near 0.0127. Higher one-attempt success can still require more source/inverse calls per accepted output. No general probability-learning, adaptive or fixed-point method is implemented.

The thermal source limits system width to four, input terms to 32, aggregate terms to 256, degree to 12 and β∑∣cj∣/2\beta\sum|c_j|/2β∑∣cj​∣/2 to 10. The amplification wrapper limits rounds to four and total wires to 24. Its 50,000 source-instruction cap is top-level; nested lowering can cost more before the 250,000-operation export cap is checked. Statevector checks stop at 12 wires and full-operator checks at six. No Lean theorem is claimed.

For standalone Python execution, save preparation_amplification.py, thermal_filter.py and lcu.py beside this notebook and use the project’s pinned Python 3.12 environment. The static site uses the saved labeled outputs without widget objects or a public kernel.
