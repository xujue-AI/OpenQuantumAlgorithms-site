Hamiltonian dynamics

Motivation and intuition¶

This chapter implements a small dynamics task with original gate construction and an independent classical answer. It belongs to the quantum-simulation umbrella; open systems and Gibbs preparation have separate tracks.

Hamiltonian simulation offers several ways to approximate the same evolution, but each produces a different mix of rotations, controlled access, arithmetic and measurements. Those operations determine which fault-tolerant architecture is worth comparing. The number of oracle calls alone cannot select a code or predict physical runtime.

Implementation status

First- and second-order product formulas implement dynamics with state, operator and observable checks. Aggregated Taylor LCU has actual PREPARE/SELECT, uncomputation and a measured/reset postselection schedule. A separate structured word-Taylor method supplies term-index access and integer-round oblivious amplification without enumerating every word. Qubitization access, a standalone degree-14 QSP cosine filter, complex postselected evolution and a separate integer-round oblivious-amplification path are checked. The computed static study supplies conditional surface-code projections for product formulas and complex QSP/OAA. Separate one-system word-Taylor and one/two-spin retargetable-QSP studies supply actual Clifford+T sequences and conditional reset-aware physical models. Aggregated Taylor and wider word-Taylor physical costing, deterministic evolution and certified synthesis remain planned. No hardware advantage or formal proof is claimed.

The fixed task¶

Use three spins on an open chain with

H=A+B,A=Z0Z1+Z1Z2,B=0.7(X0+X1+X2).H=A+B,\quad A=Z_0Z_1+Z_1Z_2,\quad B=0.7(X_0+X_1+X_2).H=A+B,A=Z0​Z1​+Z1​Z2​,B=0.7(X0​+X1​+X2​).
(1)

The coupling J=1J=1J=1 defines the energy unit, ℏ=1\hbar=1ℏ=1, and time is t=1t=1t=1 in inverse coupling units. Prepare ∣ψ0⟩=∣000⟩|\psi_0\rangle=|000\rangle∣ψ0​⟩=∣000⟩. The state task approximates U(t)∣ψ0⟩U(t)|\psi_0\rangleU(t)∣ψ0​⟩ for U(t)=e−iHtU(t)=e^{-iHt}U(t)=e−iHt; the observable task estimates ⟨Z0⟩\langle Z_0\rangle⟨Z0​⟩ after evolution. These outputs have different repetition costs. The exact small classical observable is approximately 0.404255857287.

The input is an explicit Pauli coefficient list. Qiskit labels read q[2] q[1] q[0]: the terms are IZZ, ZZI, IIX, IXI, XII. The observable assigns +1 to bit 0 equal to zero and -1 to bit 0 equal to one. The zero basis input needs no preparation gates; a device still needs initialization. No external data loading is modeled.

For an approximation VVV, report spectral operator error ∥V−U∥2\|V-U\|_2∥V−U∥2​, state-vector error ∥Vψ0−Uψ0∥2\|V\psi_0-U\psi_0\|_2∥Vψ0​−Uψ0​∥2​, state infidelity and observable bias separately. Vector error retains phase; infidelity does not. For normalized states, a state-vector error ϵ\epsilonϵ bounds this observable bias by 2ϵ2\epsilon2ϵ.

Fundamental method: product formulas¶

The Hamiltonian terms are easy to exponentiate individually even when their sum is not. Product formulas alternate those simple rotations. Shorter steps reduce the mismatch caused by noncommutation; a symmetric schedule cancels the leading splitting error. The fixed example below makes that error visible against exact small-system evolution.

Let δ=t/r\delta=t/rδ=t/r. Applying an AAA layer followed chronologically by a BBB layer gives

V1(t;r)=(e−iBδe−iAδ)r.V_1(t;r)=\left(e^{-iB\delta}e^{-iA\delta}\right)^r.V1​(t;r)=(e−iBδe−iAδ)r.
(2)

Applying half an AAA layer, a full BBB layer and half an AAA layer gives

V2(t;r)=(e−iAδ/2e−iBδe−iAδ/2)r.V_2(t;r)=\left(e^{-iA\delta/2}e^{-iB\delta}e^{-iA\delta/2}\right)^r.V2​(t;r)=(e−iAδ/2e−iBδe−iAδ/2)r.
(3)

These are Lie–Trotter and symmetric Strang splittings Trotter, 1959Strang, 1968. Modern commutator bounds explain their approximation error Childs et al., 2021; the curves here are numerical results for this instance.

Terms within each layer commute. Each e−icδZiZi+1e^{-ic\delta Z_iZ_{i+1}}e−icδZi​Zi+1​ uses CX–RZ(2cδ)R_Z(2c\delta)RZ​(2cδ)–CX; each e−icδXie^{-ic\delta X_i}e−icδXi​ uses RX(2cδ)R_X(2c\delta)RX​(2cδ). Half layers halve the angle. The generator uses these primitive operations directly and never appends a dense evolution gate.

Taylor LCU with actual access¶

For a segment δ=t/r\delta=t/rδ=t/r, form

TK(δ)=∑k=0K(−iδH)kk!=∑jbjPj,s=∑j∣bj∣.T_K(\delta)=\sum_{k=0}^K\frac{(-i\delta H)^k}{k!}=\sum_j b_j P_j,\qquad s=\sum_j|b_j|.TK​(δ)=k=0∑K​k!(−iδH)k​=j∑​bj​Pj​,s=j∑​∣bj​∣.
(4)

Bounded classical preprocessing multiplies Pauli strings, combines identical strings and removes only exact floating-point zeros. Its actual normalization differs from the unaggregated word-series normalization. This preprocessing can grow exponentially; it is not the scalable word-indexed access model of the truncated-Taylor algorithm Berry et al., 2015.

A binary tree of controlled RYR_YRY​ rotations implements

PREPARE∣0⟩=∑j∣bj∣/s∣j⟩.\mathrm{PREPARE}|0\rangle=\sum_j\sqrt{|b_j|/s}|j\rangle.PREPARE∣0⟩=j∑​∣bj​∣/s​∣j⟩.
(5)

Indexed X/Y/Z gates and coefficient phases implement SELECT=∑j∣j⟩⟨j∣⊗eiarg⁡bjPj\mathrm{SELECT}=\sum_j|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes e^{i\arg b_j}P_jSELECT=∑j​∣j⟩⟨j∣⊗eiargbj​Pj​. Padded labels apply identity. PREPARE, SELECT and inverse PREPARE give a unitary whose zero-label block is TK/sT_K/sTK​/s. Tests compare that actual block with an independent polynomial matrix, including negative/complex coefficients and bit ordering.

Important variant: structured word-Taylor access¶

Structured word-Taylor dynamics prepares a degree register and one independent Hamiltonian-term register per possible word slot. Signed SELECT operations activate the selected slots and then uncompute the access registers. The construction follows the word-access viewpoint of truncated-Taylor simulation Berry et al., 2015; it supplies gates without constructing the exponentially long classical word table.

The method page checks the actual encoded polynomial, conditional state and acceptance against independent matrix references on a two-spin input. It also implements integer-round oblivious amplification and an explicit zero-LCU padding variant with normalization two. The chosen input, approximation and acceptance remain visible: padding and amplification do not turn the finite Taylor polynomial into exact deterministic evolution. Its complete measured-trial ledger, bounded size study and notebook belong to that method page.

Important variant: qubitization and QSP¶

Qubitization and quantum signal processing has its own method page beneath Hamiltonian dynamics. Actual Pauli access encodes H/αH/\alphaH/α; reproducible even/odd phases and a selector produce a checked zero-ancilla block near U/4U/4U/4. The page includes a three-wire polynomial circuit, the fixed dynamics results, in-page cost and verification analysis, and an executed size sweep at fixed αt\alpha tαt. A separate bounded physical study retargets phases for two, three and four spins at fixed local couplings and time. General size-dependent degree and phase generation remain planned.

The oblivious-amplification variant adds actual forward/inverse access and zero-ancilla reflections. Its success remains probabilistic and retry accounting assumes a freshly re-preparable input. Deterministic evolution and certified synthesis remain planned. The standalone ledgers use different output contracts; the static study below instead fixes the observable accuracy and failure allocations before comparing complete measured workloads.

Fundamental methods and output contracts¶

An algorithm should be chosen for a specified output and an implemented Hamiltonian representation. Approximating the full evolution operator, estimating one observable, and supplying controlled evolution to phase estimation are different tasks. An error bound for one does not automatically serve the others. The number of spins alone is also insufficient: simulated time, term magnitudes, access construction, requested error and confidence change the workload.

Commutator-aware analysis is the appropriate starting point for product formulas Childs et al., 2021. qDRIFT provides a distinct randomized method Campbell, 2019. Writing H=∑jhjHjH=\sum_j h_j H_jH=∑j​hj​Hj​ with ∥Hj∥≤1\|H_j\|\leq1∥Hj​∥≤1 and λ=∑j∣hj∣\lambda=\sum_j|h_j|λ=∑j​∣hj​∣, its elementary-evolution count scales as O(λ2t2/ϵ)O(\lambda^2t^2/\epsilon)O(λ2t2/ϵ) in the averaged-channel error model. This is not a guarantee that one fixed seeded circuit has operator error ϵ\epsilonϵ. The original paper explicitly distinguishes those errors. A small demo must therefore compare both individual trajectories and the channel or measured ensemble, retaining its independently sampled circuits and seeds.

Corrected product formulas add corrector operations to a product formula for two exactly simulatable partitions Bagherimehrab et al., 2026. In the analyzed symplectic construction, inverse correctors cancel between adjacent steps, leaving boundary work. A compiled comparison must retain those boundary gates and the error of implementing the corrector itself. The perturbed-system results also assume a small-norm partition. The paper’s spectral-norm bounds and lattice experiments support a planned variant; they do not provide resources for this book’s circuits or a replacement for an application’s energy-estimation error contract.

Multi-product formulas deserve two separate variants. Coherent multi-product simulation uses a linear combination of product-formula circuits Low et al., 2019; that source remains a preprint with no peer-reviewed successor found in the checks recorded here. The published hardware-friendly variant classically combines expectation values from different circuits Vazquez et al., 2023. It supplies an observable estimate rather than a prepared evolved state or a coherent controlled evolution. Coefficient conditioning and sampling variance belong in its end-to-end cost.

The system-size analysis also needs a bounded commutator argument. Mizuta’s 2026 result explains why an earlier bound involving arbitrarily high nested commutators does not establish locality-based size scaling, and supplies a finite-truncation alternative Mizuta, 2026. The fixed two-term companion uses a conservative finite second-order certificate alongside its observed fourth-order cancellation. Optimized high-order bounds and physical comparisons remain planned. This analysis paper is not a publication successor of the original coherent-MPF preprint.

The existing Taylor and QSP pages provide actual access constructions Berry et al., 2015Low & Chuang, 2019Low & Chuang, 2017. Their query-complexity improvements do not make an expensive coefficient loader or SELECT circuit free. For Hamiltonians with a cheaply exponentiated large component, interaction-picture simulation can change the relevant norm dependence. The original method is a preprint reference Low & Wiebe, 2018; the published hybrid framework explains both its opportunities and substantial constant-factor/access tradeoffs Rajput et al., 2022. This is a separate implementation track rather than a relabeling of the current QSP circuit.

Martyn and Rall’s stochastic QSP replaces one polynomial with a probabilistic ensemble. Under their exponential Chebyshev-coefficient decay condition, a degree-ddd deterministic approximation can be replaced by expected degree d/2+O(1)d/2+O(1)d/2+O(1) while retaining the same order of averaged-channel error Martyn & Rall, 2025. The constants and any postselection normalization still matter for equal-accuracy comparison; an individual sampled circuit does not inherit the ensemble’s error guarantee. The paper also studies imaginary-time evolution, phase estimation and matrix inversion. Its Fig. 2 code is offered by request from the authors. No upstream code, stochastic circuit, phase ensemble or physical comparison is implemented here. A future companion must expose the sampled circuits and independently check the channel, including acceptance, repeated preparation and readout costs.

Space and time scaling¶

For the nnn-spin local chain, one first-order step has nnn X rotations and n−1n-1n−1 ZZ rotations, each ZZ using two CX gates. Before compilation, rrr steps therefore have (2n−1)r(2n-1)r(2n−1)r rotations and 2(n−1)r2(n-1)r2(n−1)r CX gates, nnn system wires and nnn final readouts. A symmetric step duplicates half of the ZZ layer; adjoining halves can be merged, so source-operation counts and compiled counts must remain distinct. The actual generator records both chosen schedule and compiled dependency depth.

At fixed rrr, construction scales linearly in nnn. Holding target accuracy fixed can require increasing rrr with nnn, time, couplings and commutators; the observed first/second-order convergence for three spins does not certify that choice for every size. Observable shots and postselection attempts multiply the complete measured circuit cost. QSP’s method page separately records degree, term-access, ancilla and normalization costs. The bounded Taylor aggregator can expand exponentially. The structured word-access variant instead records the degree and term registers, actual controlled access, primitive materialization limits and complete attempts; its bounded size study remains separate from an equal-accuracy application scaling claim.

A fault-tolerant code is not selected by the algorithm name alone. The implementation exposes a workload: arbitrary-angle Pauli rotations, Toffoli/CCZ operations, controls, temporary registers, measurements, feedforward and spatial transport. Those primitives determine which architecture is worth compiling and measuring. The current surface-code baseline remains the common reference; the alternatives in this section have not been executed or compared with that baseline on this site.

The current evidence includes separate one-system word-Taylor and one/two-spin retargetable-QSP instrument models with actual Clifford+T sequences and charged reset/readout. Their numerical synthesis guards and conditional physical scenarios remain distinct from the historical static PSSPC mean-fit projections. They supply bounded implementation evidence; they do not evaluate the alternative architectures in the following table.

The appropriate lesson from comparative work is conditional. Childs and coauthors found QSP preferable among their algorithms with rigorous performance guarantees, while higher-order product formulas prevailed when empirical error estimates sufficed Childs et al., 2018. Their compiled spin-system study does not establish a universal winner. Likewise, the 2025 STAR compilation paper reports a note-added update, based on private communication, that a Clifford+T/qubitization estimate at lower physical error may have better or comparable performance Akahoshi et al., 2025. That reported update is not an independently reproduced physical estimate here.

STAR deliberately retains partially corrected analog-rotation noise. Increasing code distance does not by itself suppress all of that injection error; correction branches and the finite rotation budget need their own model Akahoshi et al., 2024. A broader transversal fault-tolerance result provides another architecture reference, with correlated decoding, magic-state input and feedforward requirements Zhou et al., 2025. Its licensed code/data archive is a prospective implementation source; it has not been downloaded or executed here. The high-rate STAR preprint extends this direction to bicycle-chain codes, but its non-scalable MLE-decoder resource model and proposed QPE extension remain explicit limitations Ismail et al., 2026. This preprint is a later planned architecture target.

For each new comparison, freeze the Hamiltonian and representation, time, input state, requested output, accuracy, success probability and physical assumptions. Record separate algorithm, compilation, synthesis, sampling and physical-error allowances. Supply concrete circuits and count every preparation, controlled operation, uncomputation, retry, readout and classical postprocessing step. Architecture-specific assumptions must be visible alongside the result. Published resource numbers for a different energy-error convention, state overlap or hardware model are reference points, not improvements over the existing baseline.

Verification and formalization¶

The implementation includes the original product-formula/LCU/cosine checks, plus independently reviewed complex-QSP, amplification, thermal and dissipative checks. The notebook executes the examples independently with a fresh kernel. These are numerical and functional checks, not formal proof certificates.

Product formulas are compared with independently assembled Pauli matrices and exact matrix exponentiation. Tests retain operator error, state error, infidelity and observable error as separate quantities. For the selected input, boundary phases make the two splitting orders produce the same Z measurement even while their state/operator errors differ. One matching observable is therefore insufficient evidence of matching evolution.

LCU tests check the actual encoded Taylor block, normalization, conditional state, acceptance probabilities and complete-attempt/capped-retry accounting. The quantum access circuits are implemented; the dense classical calculation is a separate reference. Its input and output contracts retain postselection.

The structured word-Taylor checks additionally retain degree/term-register ordering, signed Hamiltonian access, inactive padded labels and the actual amplification recurrence. Its normalization-two variant includes explicit zero-LCU padding. Conditional state error, unnormalized operator error and complete-attempt acceptance are checked separately; a high acceptance probability does not remove polynomial bias.

QSP checks compare the circuit block with an independent spectral cosine function, verify scalar/Chebyshev response and frozen phase reproduction, and test additional Hermitian instances. The small degree-two circuit checks 2H2−I2H^2-I2H2−I independently. The fixed-αt\alpha tαt size sweep checks full operators only through three data spins; four- and five-spin checks cover the selected zero input. Separately, the fixed-coupling resource study reconstructs every accepted-block basis column for its two- and four-spin retargeted circuits, including the nine-wire four-spin circuit, and checks a seeded full-register input after lowering. Numerical coefficient reconstruction, trajectory calculations and sampled grids are not formal interval or compiler certificates. The separate complex-QSP and amplification checks retain relative branch phases, compare actual accepted blocks and measure state-dependent acceptance. Integer amplification remains probabilistic; these checks do not establish deterministic evolution or certified synthesis.

The static study checks every exported QASM hash against the costed circuit, all final readouts, capped attempts, synthesis allocations and modeled physical union bounds. Exact source/environment hashes and five unchanged Grover-baseline hashes appear in its manifest. These checks validate the reported workload and model arithmetic, while the excluded costs and uncertified synthesis assumptions remain explicit.

A separate 32-spin generation run produced circuits and metadata while explicitly reporting simulation as unperformed. Materialization and dense-reference limits are separate; a large circuit export does not demonstrate scalable classical simulation.

Classical simulation and formalization¶

The following requirements apply to the planned comparisons above.

Small unitary methods will be compared with an independently constructed matrix exponential, while randomized or reset-based methods need a full channel or ensemble reference. Statevector and density-operator simulations have separate bounds; a scalable circuit generator does not make either simulation efficient.

The cited algorithm and architecture results are external mathematical or resource-model evidence. No Lean or Rocq proof has been reproduced for these planned comparisons. A future formal artifact must state its exact theorem, assumptions, pinned environment and connection to the generated gates.
