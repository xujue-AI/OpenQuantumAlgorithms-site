Fixed-point amplitude amplification

Motivation and intuition¶

Ordinary Grover search can overshoot: repeating its two reflections past the success peak reduces success again. Fixed-point amplitude amplification uses a sequence of different reflection phases to obtain high ideal success throughout a promised range of initial probabilities. Yoder, Low and Chuang give the phase sequence and its Chebyshev success law Yoder et al., 2014.

Implemented and checked scope

The preparation, inverse preparation, good-register phase and complete all-zero phase are actual gates. The three-wire example and five selected sizes are simulated and checked against an independent polynomial recurrence; small full operators retain their global phase in a separate projector-matrix check. Generated circuits and conditional surface-code projections are downloadable. The ideal theorem does not certify binary64 angles, a concrete Clifford+T synthesis, hardware confidence or quantum advantage.

Physical initialization/reset, external loading and classical verification/controller/decoder latency are excluded. The input model assumes clean logical zero qubits and a supplied coherent preparation circuit.

Task, input model and fundamental algorithm¶

Supply a known unitary preparation AAA on qqq wires, so ∣s⟩=A∣0q⟩|s\rangle=A|0^q\rangle∣s⟩=A∣0q⟩, and a computational-basis projector Πg\Pi_gΠg​ identifying good outputs. Initial success is p=⟨s∣Πg∣s⟩p=\langle s|\Pi_g|s\ranglep=⟨s∣Πg​∣s⟩. The schedule accepts a promise p≥w>0p\ge w>0p≥w>0 and a failure amplitude 0<δ<10<\delta<10<δ<1; it does not measure or compute exact ppp to choose phases. The requested ideal output failure probability is at most δ2\delta^2δ2.

This interface requires reusable AAA and A†A^\daggerA†. An unknown specimen supplied once cannot replace that access model. Work qubits used by preparation belong to the coherent register; the reflection about the initial zero state must cover them too. The general amplitude-amplification framework motivates this known-preparation model Brassard et al., 2002.

The actual projector phase is

Sg(β)=I+(eiβ−1)Πg.S_g(\beta)=I+(e^{i\beta}-1)\Pi_g.Sg​(β)=I+(eiβ−1)Πg​.
(1)

With S0(ϕ)=I+(eiϕ−1)∣0q⟩⟨0q∣S_0(\phi)=I+(e^{i\phi}-1)|0^q\rangle\langle0^q|S0​(ϕ)=I+(eiϕ−1)∣0q⟩⟨0q∣, our convention is

Gj=−AS0(−αj)A†Sg(βj).G_j=-A S_0(-\alpha_j)A^\dagger S_g(\beta_j).Gj​=−AS0​(−αj​)A†Sg​(βj​).
(2)

After the initial preparation, round jjj chronologically applies Sg(βj)S_g(\beta_j)Sg​(βj​), A†A^\daggerA†, S0(−αj)S_0(-\alpha_j)S0​(−αj​), AAA, and global phase π\piπ. Round one acts first. Preserving the global minus sign matters for coherent reuse, even though it does not affect final measured probabilities.

Phase schedule and complexity¶

Let L=2ℓ+1L=2\ell+1L=2ℓ+1 and

γ=sech⁡[acosh⁡(1/δ)L].\gamma=\operatorname{sech}\left[\frac{\operatorname{acosh}(1/\delta)}L\right].γ=sech[Lacosh(1/δ)​].
(3)

The phases are

αj=−βℓ−j+1=2arccot⁡[tan⁡(2πj/L)1−γ2],j=1,…,ℓ.\alpha_j=-\beta_{\ell-j+1} =2\operatorname{arccot}\left[\tan(2\pi j/L)\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}\right],\quad j=1,\ldots,\ell.αj​=−βℓ−j+1​=2arccot[tan(2πj/L)1−γ2​],j=1,…,ℓ.
(4)

The ideal success law is

PL(p)=1−δ2TL(1−pγ)2,P_L(p)=1-\delta^2 T_L\left(\frac{\sqrt{1-p}}\gamma\right)^2,PL​(p)=1−δ2TL​(γ1−p​​)2,
(5)

where TLT_LTL​ is the degree-LLL Chebyshev polynomial. For p≥1−γ2p\ge1-\gamma^2p≥1−γ2, its argument lies in [0,1][0,1][0,1], so ideal failure is at most δ2\delta^2δ2. Choose a positive odd length satisfying

L≥acosh⁡(1/δ)atanh⁡w.L\ge\frac{\operatorname{acosh}(1/\delta)}{\operatorname{atanh}\sqrt w}.L≥atanhw​acosh(1/δ)​.
(6)

The w=1w=1w=1 case needs only the initial preparation. The bounded generator chooses the smallest permitted odd length and numerically rechecks its width. It computes the phase factor as tanh⁡[acosh⁡(1/δ)/L]\tanh[\operatorname{acosh}(1/\delta)/L]tanh[acosh(1/δ)/L] to avoid subtracting nearly equal numbers. Phases remain binary64 values, without interval enclosures Yoder et al., 2014.

For small www and δ\deltaδ, the preparation-access scaling is O(log⁡(1/δ)/w)O(\log(1/\delta)/\sqrt w)O(log(1/δ)/w​). Actual calls here are ℓ+1\ell+1ℓ+1 forward AAA and ℓ\ellℓ inverse A†A^\daggerA†, totaling LLL, with ℓ\ellℓ good phases and ℓ\ellℓ full zero phases. The paper’s L−1L-1L−1 bit-oracle query convention counts compute/uncompute within each good phase; it must not be equated with these A/A†A/A^\daggerA/A† calls.

“Fixed point” describes an expanding guaranteed success band as the full schedule is recomputed. Individual phases are not prefixes of a longer schedule, and success need not increase pointwise with LLL. Inputs below the promise still have a defined circuit and success curve, but lose the uniform guarantee. δ=0\delta=0δ=0 has no finite uniform zero-error schedule for p<1p<1p<1.

End-to-end resource analysis¶

For one complete trial, with the actual preparation and projectors at size nnn,

For RRR fresh serial trials, every term is paid RRR times. This release uses R=1R=1R=1. No oracle, preparation, inverse or multi-controlled reflection is assigned unit gate cost. A different reversible application predicate or loading circuit changes these costs and must be explicitly supplied. Here nnn means data bits, and live width is q=n+1q=n+1q=n+1 including the flag.

The logical report records preparation calls, both projectors, all final readouts, primitive counts and dependency depth. The complete primitive circuit retains arbitrary rotations. The resource adapter additionally optimizes that same complete trial at level one; its estimated circuit has its own hash and a checked ideal output. OpenQASM’s omitted overall phase is irrelevant to this measured trial; the export is not a controlled-oracle specification.

Surface-code model and failure contract¶

The computed physical reports use the audited Microsoft QDK 1.34.0 adapter Microsoft Quantum Development Kit contributors, n.d.. Its two fixed profiles have physical error rates 10-3 and 10-4, 100 ns gates and 500 ns measurements, with rotated surface code and round-based magic-state factories. The existing Grover baseline remains unchanged.

The default canonical QDK trial trace contains 91 exact T states, 48 arbitrary rotations and three measurements. PSSPC assigns 17 T states per rotation, using a summed synthesis-model scalar about 6.11×10−66.11\times10^{-6}6.11×10−6, within the configured 10-5 allowance. These are modeled mean synthesis costs, without a generated per-angle Clifford+T sequence, approximation certificate or runtime-tail guarantee.

Allocate ideal failure δ2=0.0025\delta^2=0.0025δ2=0.0025, synthesis-model allowance 10-5 and physical-fault allowance 0.005. Their sum, 0.00751, is a conditional one-trial failure ceiling only if exact phases meet the theorem, the synthesis scalar upper-bounds output-distribution total variation, the physical model bounds faults and classical verification is correct. Binary64 phase evaluation supplies no rigorous angle certificate. Certified hardware confidence remains unavailable.

The default fastest returned point at physical error 10-4 uses 14,308 peak physical qubits and 4.28 ms modeled serial runtime; at 10-3 it uses 175,452 qubits and 7.704 ms. The five-size sweep returns 52 Pareto points across the two profiles. All points, code distances, factories and model provenance are retained; the plot selects each size/profile’s fastest returned point.

Peak qubits times runtime is a rectangular space-time proxy, not an integral of changing occupancy. Clifford scheduling is abstracted by the estimator. Physical initialization/reset, data loading, controller/decoder/verification latency and synthesis runtime tails remain outside the measured boundary; these projections cannot establish an end-to-end application advantage. All physical scaling points are downloadable.

Reproduce, simulate and inspect¶

The original implementation uses Qiskit core 2.6.2 for gates, statevectors, lowering and exports Qiskit contributors, n.d.; NumPy 2.5.4 supplies independent matrix/recurrence arithmetic NumPy developers, n.d., and Matplotlib 3.11.2 creates the original figures Matplotlib developers, n.d.. Package versions, source/generator/lock hashes, CPU device and transpiler seed are recorded. The algorithm code and circuit figures are original; upstream algorithm code is not copied.

python scripts/generate_fixed_point_amplification.py --estimate
python -m pytest tests/test_fixed_point_amplification.py

To generate larger circuits without statevector execution:

python scripts/generate_fixed_point_amplification.py --data-qubits 8 \
  --threshold 64 --no-sim --no-scaling --output-dir artifacts/fixed-point-n8

The source builder accepts at most 12 live wires, L≤255L\le255L≤255, a 200,000 recursive source/expansion preflight and definition nesting depth at most 64. Primitive exports are capped at 250,000 operations after lowering; this is a guard, not a compilation-runtime proof. Statevector probability checks are separately capped at ten live wires. Tiny failure amplitudes whose squared bound underflows binary64 are rejected. The generator removes stale optional physical/scaling artifacts when invocation changes.

The notebook includes bounded live controls that regenerate actual circuits and recheck probabilities. Saved outputs work on a static site. A live local or Binder kernel enables the controls; it is not required for public reading. The notebook also runs beside the fixed-point module and preparation module, using the scientific dependencies from the Binder environment. Estimation additionally needs the full locked environment.

Verification and formalization¶

The dedicated tests check independent Chebyshev recurrence values, exact good-register bit ordering, complete complex operators and global phases, lowered-state equivalence, conditional good-state direction, final measurement mapping, invalid/noncoherent preparation and generation/simulation bounds. These are deterministic numerical and functional checks over selected instances. The executable notebook and generation reports record which simulations ran; an unexecuted export has no simulation badge.

No Lean formalization, proof-producing phase generator, verified compiler or certified hardware implementation is integrated. The paper’s mathematical theorem is a reference, and numerical agreement does not prove this Python implementation for every allowed input.
