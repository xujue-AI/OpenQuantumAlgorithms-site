Retained targets and recycled clocks

Motivation and fundamental construction¶

The energy protocol needs several phase measurements conditional on the same energy component. Retaining the data while resetting a clock supplies those measurements with fewer live wires. HF is prepared once per group; it is not reloaded for every round.

build_recycled_clock_qpe explicitly resets data at group start, prepares X(q0) per dimer, then resets mmm clock wires before each QPE round. Each measured clock output occupies its own classical bit segment. No measurement-dependent change is applied to the retained Hamiltonian data.

The diagram is a small three-round, two-clock-bit instrument. Its resets and intermediate measurements are actual operations.

End-to-end resource analysis¶

Fresh-clock static width is n+Rmn+Rmn+Rm; recycling uses n+mn+mn+m. With the defaults n=2n=2n=2, m=5m=5m=5 and R=19R=19R=19, that is 97 versus 7. The recycled group has n+Rm=97n+Rm=97n+Rm=97 explicit source resets and 95 clock readouts, one HF preparation and 589 repeated U calls. All K=2K=2K=2 groups still run; recycling does not remove confidence or preparation work.

The reset ledger separates source/primitive gates, resets and output bits. Dynamic lowering preserves initial resets rather than deleting them under an implicit-zero assumption. Reset faults, preparation timing, reuse/feedback communication and physical scheduling need an instrument-aware backend. The current static QDK importer rejects this circuit; its physical estimate is unavailable.

Analytic powers can also replace repeated U calls on this known family. One-ancilla feedback QPE has a further width tradeoff, introduced in the QPE chapter; it is not silently included in this mmm-clock protocol.

Verification and formalization¶

Small actual measurement/reset circuits and their primitive lowering are compared with complete deferred-clock output distributions and independent spectral-mixture laws. Conditional target collapse preserves the joint energy law. Measuring the target or repreparing HF within a group would change that law.

The canonical sequential simulator applies gates and projects/resets measured clocks with a fixed seed. It verifies bounded output behavior without allocating the full deferred register. No synthesized physical instrument or Lean proof is claimed. Measured reset circuit and the notebook expose the implementation.
