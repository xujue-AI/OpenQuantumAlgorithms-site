Portability with fixed starts

The finite-shot examples couple three sampling seeds to different initial angles. Here we reuse the same circuit generators and sampled polling rule on two changed inputs, while holding each task’s initial angles fixed across seeds. This separates sampling-seed variation from initialization changes within these runs. It tests a small amount of implementation portability rather than large-instance performance of QAOA or VQE Farhi et al., 2014Peruzzo et al., 2014.

Fixed experiment and evidence

The three seeds, initial angles, 32-sweep schedule, measurement groups, and post-training targets are declared before the runs. Results are reported for every seed, including target misses. The inputs and schedule are not retuned after seeing outcomes.

Counts are ideal samples drawn classically from explicit circuit probabilities. Exact reference values are post-training diagnostics. A successful toy run does not establish convergence, a hardware success probability, or an advantage over the independent classical solution.

A triangle MaxCut input¶

Use three vertices and edges

E△={(0,1),(1,2),(0,2)}.\mathcal E_\triangle=\{(0,1),(1,2),(0,2)\}.E△​={(0,1),(1,2),(0,2)}.
(1)

Exhaustive enumeration of its eight assignments gives cut values [0,2,2,2,2,2,2,0][0,2,2,2,2,2,2,0][0,2,2,2,2,2,2,0] in integer bitstring order. The classical maximum is two. Any assignment with one vertex separated from the other two achieves it; 000 and 111 have cut zero. Qiskit strings read q2q1q0q_2q_1q_0q2​q1​q0​ with wire zero least significant.

The one-layer QAOA generator prepares ∣+⟩⊗3|+\rangle^{\otimes3}∣+⟩⊗3 with H gates, uses CX; RZ(-gamma); CX for each edge, and uses RX(2*beta) for the mixer. The minimized objective remains the negative mean cut. An all-Z outcome scores all three edges jointly. The requested post-training exact mean is at least 1.90, a gap at most 0.10 from this input’s classical maximum two. It is separate from the probability of sampling a maximum-cut bitstring.

Reuse the protocol and vary only the seed¶

Every QAOA run starts at (γ,β)=(0.2,0.1)(\gamma,\beta)=(0.2,0.1)(γ,β)=(0.2,0.1) and every VQE run starts at θ=0.6\theta=0.6θ=0.6. Seeds are 7, 19, and 41. NumPy SeedSequence uses algorithm IDs two and three for these portability tasks, keeping their streams separate from IDs zero and one used in the original tasks NumPy Developers, n.d.. Training and fresh validation also use distinct stream families. Within each task, the supplied input, initial point, optimizer settings, and measurement allocation stay fixed as the seed changes.

The initial call uses 256 shots per group. Four eight-sweep blocks use coordinate step/shot pairs (0.4,256),(0.2,512),(0.1,1024),(0.05,2048)(0.4,256),(0.2,512),(0.1,1024),(0.05,2048)(0.4,256),(0.2,512),(0.1,1024),(0.05,2048). Every sweep samples a fresh incumbent and all negative/positive coordinate proposals. A move requires the sampled reduction to exceed twice the combined descriptive standard error. The only stopping rule is schedule exhaustion. The finite-shot chapter details why this adaptive rule is not a calibrated statistical test or convergence certificate.

Every final state receives a fresh 4,096-shot-per-group holdout. This separate readout task asks for absolute expectation error at most 0.10 at 95% confidence for one fixed estimate. The bounded-score radii are about 0.0424 for triangle cuts and 0.0618 for the second energy, supporting that readout allocation under the ideal independent-sampling model Hoeffding, 1963.

These bands refer to the expectation at the selected angles. They are not optimizer-success bounds, and six individually 95% intervals are not jointly 95%. Exact post-training quality and finite-shot estimates are shown separately. The VQE holdout band is wider than the requested 0.03 ground-energy gap; supporting the separate 0.10 expectation-estimation task does not certify that ground-energy target.

This portability check applies the same bounded sampled optimizer to two new toy inputs: the three-vertex triangle MaxCut graph with all three edges, and the Hamiltonian H = -0.8 Z0 - 0.6 Z1 + 0.4 X0X1 with the same RY/CX ansatz. All eight classical cut assignments give optimum 2; full matrix diagonalization gives ground energy -sqrt(2.12). Changed problem inputs prevent a same-task resource comparison with earlier reports.

These runs optimize sampled objectives. Ideal measurement outcomes are generated from statevector probabilities; exact expectations are used only for post-decision diagnostics. They are modeled ideal measurements, rather than executed hardware shots.

The bounded schedule contains four blocks of eight sweeps: (step radians, shots/group) = (0.4, 256), (0.2, 512), (0.1, 1024), (0.05, 2048). The initial objective uses 256 shots/group. Each sweep freshly measures its incumbent and both coordinate proposals for every parameter, including all rejected proposals. A candidate is accepted only when the sampled decrease exceeds twice the combined estimated standard error; this margin is a heuristic, without a confidence or convergence guarantee across adaptive choices. Every run stops after the prescribed 32 sweeps.

Seeds 7, 19 and 41 use the same fixed initialization for each task: QAOA (0.2, 0.1), VQE (0.6). Only the seeded measurement randomness changes within each task. Three observed runs do not certify a population success probability. No target-based retuning is performed. Fresh validation uses 4,096 shots per group and a separate keyed random-stream family after training ends.

Simulator/probability generation and classical search work are software timings rather than quantum runtime. Logical counts repeat every preparation, measurement basis change, and readout for all training and validation shots. Arbitrary rotations remain unsynthesized. Record the complete workload and comparison limits¶

The schedule uses 161 QAOA training calls and 97 VQE training calls per seed. All proposals, incumbent remeasurements, the initial call, and fresh final holdout count. Across the three seeds, the circuit protocol represents 473,856 QAOA shots and 579,072 VQE shots, totaling 1,052,928. The resource ledger maps every measured group and its basis changes, gates, and qubit readout. These protocol totals are not measured hardware consumption; the implementation generates counts classically.

The original four-vertex cycle and first Hamiltonian remain separate baseline cases. A smaller triangle uses fewer qubits and edges, but it is a different problem with a different objective maximum and target. The two Hamiltonians also have different coefficients and energy scales. The original runs vary initialization, and the new cases use separate random streams; comparing equal seed labels across experiments mixes input, initialization, and random-stream changes. Equal optimizer schedules do not turn their logical counts or empirical target outcomes into matched-problem physical improvements. No claim of quantum advantage, transferable convergence, or a general success rate follows from these comparisons.

The complete recorded-trajectory resource replay concerns the original tasks. It is not a physical projection for these new inputs. Portability results here contain logical workload and measured local CPU effort; their physical resources remain planned. CPU timings of simulation and classical control are kept separate from unmeasured quantum gate, reset, routing, and feedback latency.
