Decoded quantum interferometry

Motivation and intuition¶

Decoded quantum interferometry (DQI) connects optimization to coding theory: a Fourier transform turns a coherently decoded state into an objective-biased distribution. Jordan and colleagues introduced it in Nature on 22 October 2025 Jordan et al., 2025. It is a separate optimization approach alongside QAOA and adiabatic evolution.

Implementation status: executed default, structured degree-one family and finite graph degree-two component; general families and physical accounting planned

Our original nine-wire default circuit passed 63 focused CPU tests and coherent checks of preparation, all 512 decoder basis mappings, cleanup and final amplitudes. The portable public notebook has also executed with a standard Python kernel: four cells completed on Linux with locked Python 3.12.14 on 13 November 2026 (3.074 seconds recorded before the final guard seal). Its cells check saved facts and file bindings and present the original figure; this later notebook run does not repeat the earlier quantum simulation.

These are separate evidence events. The earlier combined circuit-and-notebook attempt stopped after 58.014051583 seconds with its notebook incomplete. Its complete circuit/export component remains valid; completion of the new public companion does not rewrite that earlier outcome. The separate structured degree-one implementation is described below. A degree-two graph-cycle variant now has an accepted finite C5 component and a separately executed saved companion, with a figure-eight export kept generation-only. Arbitrary DQI matrices and growing-degree families, rotation synthesis, general physical accounting and local formal proof remain planned. No hardware experiment or generic optimization advantage is claimed.

Fundamental method and variants¶

For binary linear constraints, let B∈F2m×nB\in\mathbb F_2^{m\times n}B∈F2m×n​ and v∈F2mv\in\mathbb F_2^mv∈F2m​. DQI prepares a low-weight superposition on a clause register, imposes signs from vvv, computes BTyB^T yBTy, reversibly decodes that syndrome to erase yyy, and applies a Hadamard transform on the assignment register. Measurement favors assignments through a chosen polynomial of the objective. Actual decoding gates and their input promise are essential parts of the algorithm Jordan et al., 2025.

The Nature paper separates algebraically structured optimal polynomial intersection from sparse max-XORSAT experiments. It reports a superpolynomial improvement over known classical algorithms for the structured problem. A tailored classical solver beats its sparse test example. The Nature result does not establish generic max-k-XORSAT advantage; neither that structured result nor decoding-step comparisons establish fault-tolerant wall-clock improvement Jordan et al., 2025.

The paper’s binary construction gives O(Tdec+m2)O(T_{\rm dec}+m^2)O(Tdec​+m2) preparation gates in its stated setting, where TdecT_{\rm dec}Tdec​ counts the reversible decoder. A classical decoder’s CPU time cannot substitute for this gate count. Higher-degree bias also requires decoding more errors Jordan et al., 2025.

Classical evaluation and sampling boundaries¶

Marwaha and colleagues study the ideal DQI distribution with known weights Marwaha et al., 2026. Their Lemma 22 permits efficient classical evaluation of an individual amplitude. Corollary 26 instead samples the full distribution approximately using an NP oracle. These are different access and complexity statements; neither establishes an ordinary efficient classical sampler for every DQI family or a quantum optimization advantage.

A useful fixed-degree classical control follows from Lemmas 14 and 22. For a normalized binary ideal DQI state with known efficiently computable coefficients and an injective map y↦BTyy\mapsto B^Tyy↦BTy on ∣y∣≤ℓ|y|\leq\ell∣y∣≤ℓ, define D=Nℓ=∑j=0ℓ(mj)D=N_\ell=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom mjD=Nℓ​=∑j=0ℓ​(jm​). Lemma 14 gives p(x)≤D/2np(x)\leq D/2^np(x)≤D/2n. Our derivation: uniformly propose an assignment and accept it with probability 2np(x)/D2^np(x)/D2np(x)/D. The accepted law is ppp and the expected number of proposals is DDD, polynomial in mmm when ℓ\ellℓ is fixed. This assumes efficient exact probability evaluation and Bernoulli acceptance; a numerical sampler requires a separate certified approximation and bit-cost analysis. For degree two, D=56D=56D=56 at m=10m=10m=10 and D=121D=121D=121 at m=15m=15m=15. These are ideal reference counts, not measured performance. No such sampler has executed here, and this ideal law does not establish correspondence to rounded circuit rotations, synthesis or a new accepted primitive pass.

Hamiltonian Decoded Quantum Interferometry for General Pauli Hamiltonians by Bu, Gu and Li is a planned variant, identified by the primary arXiv:2601.18773v1 record, submitted January 26, 2026 Bu et al., 2026. HDQI here means Hamiltonian decoded quantum interferometry. Its abstract discusses mixed-state preparation shaped by a chosen polynomial of a general Pauli Hamiltonian, assuming access to an appropriate decoding oracle. The polynomial describes the prepared state, not an unconditional polynomial-time guarantee. No local HDQI oracle or circuit has been implemented, and our binary default decoder does not validate that oracle assumption.

Planned OPI, decoder and streaming extensions¶

The following three methods use preprint versions; peer-reviewed successors are unverified. They are planned extensions here, with relevant manuscript passages read but no local implementation or physical-resource comparison. The bibliography records exact versions and bounded successor checks.

Rosmanis’s version 1 improves optimal polynomial intersection (OPI) preparation and decoding under an explicit memory model Rosmanis, 2026. Section 2 distinguishes quantum access to classical memory (QRAMC) from addressable quantum memory (QRAMQ). A nearly-linear runtime in that model still requires input loading, addressable workspace, reversible decoding, cleanup and readout. Those operations need concrete implementations and costs before comparison with an ordinary circuit or a fault-tolerant architecture; this result does not supply an estimate for our nine-wire example.

Apte and colleagues’ version 2 analyzes nonbinary cycle codes and imperfect decoding for fixed targets Apte et al., 2026. Worst-case minimum distance and recovery under DQI’s error distribution are different guarantees. The paper’s regular-graph comparisons concern specified fields, noise distributions and classical decoder classes. Keep those regimes attached to its results: they neither establish general DQI advantage nor constitute a blanket dequantization of the Nature paper’s distinct OPI construction.

Wu and Yang’s version 2 uses a streaming input contract Wu & Yang, 2026. At the stated 93.301% approximation point, Theorem 1.1 gives ordinary OPI a one-pass quantum algorithm with O~(N)\widetilde O(\sqrt N)O(N​) workspace and polynomial total time. Theorem 1.2 instead gives a specified Hermite OPI family polylogarithmic workspace and per-entry processing time; its constraints concern values and derivatives. Here NNN is stream length, so per-entry time must still be charged across the stream. Input passes, answer generation and quantum versus classical workspace belong in a comparison at the same quality target. These streaming theorems do not give physical qubits or hardware runtime for our circuit family.

Portable companion and generator¶

The executed notebook uses a normal Python kernel to check saved records, show the eight outcomes and display the figure. The portable bundle manifest binds matching source files and notebook cell sources while allowing fresh notebook execution outputs. Save the complete bundle together: QASM, validated component record, figure, module, normal generator, saved-data verifier, environment lock, MIT license, and notebook copy.

From the complete downloaded directory, check saved evidence with:

python verify_dqi_saved.py .

The verify_saved_bundle(directory) API checks the bundle’s hashes, saved primitive chronology, scalar references and notebook source binding using Python’s standard library. It constructs no circuit and performs no quantum simulation. Thus a successful companion run verifies the preserved computation’s public presentation and bindings; it does not create another 512-column quantum check.

To materialize another bounded circuit in a new directory, use the pinned Python 3.12 environment:

python generate_optimization_dqi.py --output-dir dqi-generated

The ordinary generator API and CLI have static source review GO. They were not freshly invoked during this notebook-only adaptation, so the adaptation supplies no measured generator performance or deadline-behavior check. The generate_dqi(output_dir, matrix=None, signs=None, ell=1, ...) API is designed to generate and export explicit primitives without simulation. Custom binary inputs must have distinct nonzero rows, n≤3n\leq3n≤3, m≤4m\leq4m≤4 and ℓ=1\ell=1ℓ=1. It rejects broader instances and reports generation-only evidence. The default CLI deadline is 10 seconds, with a 30-second maximum; the API checks its deadline between bounded phases. Separate generator output is not the saved validated bundle and does not validate a new instance’s quantum probability law.

Larger families and formal coverage¶

The current n≤3,m≤4,ℓ=1n\leq3,m\leq4,\ell=1n≤3,m≤4,ℓ=1 truth-table generator does not fulfill general large-DQI coverage. The separate structured degree-one family, B=[In;1nT]B=[I_n;\mathbf1_n^T]B=[In​;1nT​], now has executed clean-workspace decoder and signed-Walsh checks at n=2,3n=2,3n=2,3, plus an executed n=8n=8n=8 generation-only example. Its scalable source generator has explicit limits; other sizes and its API have no fresh runtime evidence. Its clean-input promise is distinct from the default’s verified arbitrary-dirty-scratch extension, whose 377/396 counts remain unchanged.

The fixed-degree-two graph construction adds a real weight-two shell, a clean two-error decoder and inline source/verification accounting. Its C5 code is affine/repetition-equivalent and has a linear classical optimum; no generic output-success or optimization-advantage claim follows. General matrices, growing-degree bias and complete physical comparisons remain planned. They need real correctable-weight/decoder promises, specified input and field representations, explicit reversible preparation/decoder/workspace construction, and matching synthesis, repetitions, readout/scoring and physical accounting. The implemented structured family’s all-zero assignment is an immediate classical optimum, so it supplies no optimization advantage claim. A bigger truth table alone is not an efficient general decoder. No local DQI theorem or Hamiltonian HDQI oracle has been reproduced.
