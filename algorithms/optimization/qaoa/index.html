QAOA: objective phases and mixing

QAOA encodes a discrete objective in phases, then mixes computational-basis amplitudes so those phases can change the output probabilities Farhi et al., 2014. A classical routine adjusts the phase and mixing angles. The quantum circuit supplies an objective distribution; finding useful angles, estimating that distribution, and returning a good assignment remain parts of the computation.

This page develops the QAOA example introduced in the optimization chapter. Its four-vertex cycle is small enough to enumerate classically and to compare every ideal objective call with an independent formula. The example checks the implementation and this circuit family on the stated input. It supplies no general optimizer-convergence or quantum-advantage claim.

Exact fitting and separate finite-shot readout¶

The first workflow uses a deterministic coordinate polling routine with initial points (0.2,0.1)(0.2,0.1)(0.2,0.1), (0.6,0.4)(0.6,0.4)(0.6,0.4), and (−0.3,0.25)(-0.3,0.25)(−0.3,0.25). The primary output is the first initialization, selected before execution. Every objective call uses an exact ideal statevector with zero shots, and is checked against the scalar cycle formula.

Each sweep evaluates negative and positive displacements along every coordinate from the same incumbent. It accepts the best improvement exceeding 10-12; otherwise it halves the step. The initial step is 0.4 radians, angles are reduced to [−π,π)[-\pi,\pi)[−π,π), and stopping is step below 10-6 radians or 128 sweeps. The complete trace retains rejected candidates as well as accepted ones. Local optimizer and classical-control times are software measurements.

Only after fitting, the primary state receives separate ideal finite-shot readout with seeds 7, 19, and 41, using 2,048 shots per seed. All edges are diagonal in the all-Z basis: each outcome scores the entire cut, so shared-edge covariance is retained in the variance of the joint score. Counts are drawn classically from statevector probabilities. These readout experiments do not feed sampled values back into the first optimizer.

The finite-shot increment performs actual sampled parameter updates and records all their shots, while portability tests a triangle input with fixed starts across seeds. Neither is a theorem about convergence on larger graphs.

Motivation and intuition¶

Moving a freshly supplied magic state to a distant data wire can cost more than the T gate it enables. When several T gates lie in one uninterrupted single-wire word, moving the data state to the magic port once and returning it after the word can reduce transport. Spectator data can be entangled: the transformation must preserve the whole operator, rather than assume clean intermediate wires.
