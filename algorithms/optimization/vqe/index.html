VQE: vary a state to lower its energy

VQE uses a classical optimizer to choose the parameters of a quantum state-preparation circuit. Each proposed state is prepared repeatedly, its Hamiltonian terms are measured, and the estimated energy becomes the optimizer’s objective Peruzzo et al., 2014. A short preparation circuit does not by itself determine the cost of finding good parameters: all proposals, measurement groups, shots, and classical work belong to the workflow.

For a Hermitian Hamiltonian HHH with lowest eigenvalue E0E_0E0​, every normalized trial state satisfies

E(θ)=⟨ψ(θ)∣H∣ψ(θ)⟩≥E0.E(\theta)=\langle\psi(\theta)|H|\psi(\theta)\rangle\ge E_0.E(θ)=⟨ψ(θ)∣H∣ψ(θ)⟩≥E0​.
(1)

The ansatz determines which states can be reached. Optimization can reduce energy only within that family, and finding its global minimum is a separate problem. Our small example chooses a family whose exact energy is easy to check. It establishes no general convergence guarantee or quantum advantage.

Optimize exactly, then sample the final state¶

The first implementation uses exact ideal statevector energies in a deterministic coordinate-polling optimizer. Its three initial angles are 0.6,−1.1,2.20.6,-1.1,2.20.6,−1.1,2.2, declared before the runs. Starting with a 0.4-radian step, each sweep evaluates both negative and positive displacements from the incumbent. It accepts the best decrease exceeding 10-12; otherwise it halves the step. Angles are reduced to [−π,π)[-\pi,\pi)[−π,π). It stops when the step falls below 10-6 radians or after 128 sweeps.

Every proposed and rejected energy evaluation uses zero measurement shots and appears in the complete run record, together with stopping reasons, achieved gaps, evaluation counts, and CPU timings. Those timings measure this classical implementation, including its statevector calculations.

The first initialization is chosen as the primary result before comparing outcomes. Its fitted angle is then evaluated in separate finite-shot experiments with seeds 7, 19, and 41, each using 2,048 shots per measurement group. Samples are drawn classically from ideal circuit probabilities; repeated QPU executions are not performed. These samples do not feed back into the exact optimizer. The finite-shot increment records a separate optimizer whose decisions actually use sampled objectives.

Measure two groups and retain covariance¶

The all-Z group scores each bitstring jointly as YZ=−Z0−Z1Y_Z=-Z_0-Z_1YZ​=−Z0​−Z1​. The all-X group applies H to both qubits before computational-basis readout and scores YX=12X0X1Y_X=\tfrac12X_0X_1YX​=21​X0​X1​. Their independently sampled means give

E^=Y‾Z+Y‾X,SE^(E^)=Var^(YZ)NZ+Var^(YX)NX.\widehat E=\overline Y_Z+\overline Y_X,\qquad \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}(\widehat E)= \sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(Y_Z)}{N_Z} +\frac{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(Y_X)}{N_X}}.E=YZ​+YX​,SE(E)=NZ​Var(YZ​)​+NX​Var(YX​)​​.
(6)

The Z terms use the same bitstrings, so the variance of their joint score retains covariance. Treating them as independent term measurements would misstate uncertainty. Both groups require a fresh ansatz preparation per shot; the H basis changes and final measurements are explicit in the all-Z and all-X circuit exports.

Across three seeds, this final-readout protocol represents 12,288 two-qubit preparations. Its sampled energy can lie below E0E_0E0​ through shot noise even though the exact expectation satisfies the variational bound. The finite-shot estimates do not certify the 10-8 ideal ground-energy target.

End-to-end resource analysis¶

For a general VQE study, let A(n,ℓ)A(n,\ell)A(n,ℓ) be the chosen ansatz’s unitary gate count, GGG its measurement-group count, Bg(n)B_g(n)Bg​(n) the basis-change gate count for group ggg, and NkgN_{kg}Nkg​ its shots at objective evaluation kkk. The circuit-operation ledger has the form

∑k=1K∑g=1GNkg [A(n,ℓ)+Bg(n)+readoutg].\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{g=1}^{G} N_{kg}\,[A(n,\ell)+B_g(n)+\text{readout}_g].k=1∑K​g=1∑G​Nkg​[A(n,ℓ)+Bg​(n)+readoutg​].
(7)

This is an accounting pattern, not a measured large-instance cost or a bound on the required number KKK of optimizer evaluations. The ansatz, grouping, requested precision, initialization, stopping rule, and classical optimizer must be specified before such an estimate becomes a workflow contract. The current demo has one RY and one CX per preparation, two groups, and explicit two-qubit readout.

The logical ledger distinguishes exact simulated objectives from the modeled sampled protocol. The conditional recorded-trajectory accounting below adds the synthesis, physical-fault, factory, and scheduling qualifications. An arbitrary-angle RY is not a validated T count, and a final circuit is not an estimate of the entire optimizer.

Verification and formalization¶

Independent checks compare explicit circuits with classical references, preserve preparation and measurement maps, and replay recorded sampled decisions from their counts. The finite-shot protocol’s fresh holdout bounds concern selected fixed states conditional on their training histories; they do not certify adaptive training queries, convergence, or physical hardware. Circuit export and bounded classical simulation have separate limits.
