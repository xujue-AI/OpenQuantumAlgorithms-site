Optimization and variational methods

QAOA alternates an objective-dependent phase with a mixer, while VQE changes a trial state to reduce its energy Farhi et al., 2014Peruzzo et al., 2014. Both are hybrid workflows: classical parameter search and repeated state preparations needed to evaluate an objective belong to the algorithm. Here we make those operations inspectable on two small problems.

Implementation and evidence

The first examples on this page use explicit Qiskit circuits, exact statevector objectives, independent classical references, and separately seeded final-readout experiments. The finite-shot optimization increment adds parameter updates driven by sampled objectives on the same tasks. The downloadable traces record every objective evaluation and all shot use. They check these particular circuits and runs; they establish no large-instance convergence guarantee or quantum advantage.

The first resource record contains logical circuit inputs and the complete demonstrated workload. Arbitrary rotations remain unsynthesized. The finite-shot increment adds conditional physical projections of a fixed final readout. The recorded-trajectory replay maps all stored training and holdout circuits, while the portability cases test changed inputs with fixed starts. Predictive physical estimates of a fresh hardware-driven adaptive optimization remain planned.

QAOA: MaxCut on a four-vertex cycle¶

Number the vertices 0,1,2,30,1,2,30,1,2,3 and supply the edge list

E={(0,1),(1,2),(2,3),(0,3)}.\mathcal E=\{(0,1),(1,2),(2,3),(0,3)\}.E={(0,1),(1,2),(2,3),(0,3)}.
(1)

For an assignment z=(z0,z1,z2,z3)z=(z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3)z=(z0​,z1​,z2​,z3​), the cut size and cost operator are

C(z)=∑(u,v)∈E[zu≠zv],C=∑(u,v)∈EI−ZuZv2.C(z)=\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal E}[z_u\ne z_v],\qquad C=\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal E}\frac{I-Z_uZ_v}{2}.C(z)=(u,v)∈E∑​[zu​=zv​],C=(u,v)∈E∑​2I−Zu​Zv​​.
(2)

The classical maximum is four, attained by the two alternating assignments. Qiskit wire q[0] is the least significant bit, so displayed strings read z3z2z1z0z_3z_2z_1z_0z3​z2​z1​z0​; the optimal strings are 0101 and 1010. Enumeration evaluates the edge predicate directly rather than consulting the quantum objective routine.

∣ψ(γ,β)⟩=e−iβ∑jXje−iγC∣+⟩⊗4.|\psi(\gamma,\beta)\rangle= e^{-i\beta\sum_j X_j}e^{-i\gamma C}|+\rangle^{\otimes4}.∣ψ(γ,β)⟩=e−iβ∑j​Xj​e−iγC∣+⟩⊗4.
(3)

The optimizer minimizes f(γ,β)=−⟨C⟩f(\gamma,\beta)=-\langle C\ranglef(γ,β)=−⟨C⟩. Angles are radians. Our gate convention is RP(α)=e−iαP/2R_P(\alpha)=e^{-i\alpha P/2}RP​(α)=e−iαP/2: the mixer uses rx(2*beta) on every vertex. For each edge, the phase circuit is cx(u,v); rz(-gamma,v); cx(u,v). This realizes e+iγZuZv/2e^{+i\gamma Z_uZ_v/2}e+iγZu​Zv​/2; the omitted identity contribution changes only the global phase. There is no dense-matrix oracle or unexplained access operation.

H=−Z0−Z1+12X0X1.H=-Z_0-Z_1+\frac12 X_0X_1.H=−Z0​−Z1​+21​X0​X1​.
(5)

H=(−2001/2001/2001/2001/2002).H=\begin{pmatrix} -2&0&0&1/2\\ 0&0&1/2&0\\ 0&1/2&0&0\\ 1/2&0&0&2 \end{pmatrix}.H=⎝⎛​−2001/2​001/20​01/200​1/2002​⎠⎞​.

Finite-shot energy evaluation uses two groups. In the all-Z group, one bitstring yields the joint value −Z0−Z1-Z_0-Z_1−Z0​−Z1​; its sample variance includes covariance of the Z terms. In the all-X group, apply H to both qubits before computational-basis measurement and score 12X0X1\tfrac12X_0X_121​X0​X1​. Adding the group means estimates total energy. The measurement protocol requires a fresh ansatz preparation for each shot, and H basis changes and final measurements appear in the exports. The implementation obtains each group’s statevector probabilities and draws its counts classically. Shot noise can put a sampled energy below E0E_0E0​ even though the exact expectation obeys the variational bound.

A complete, deterministic parameter search¶

The original optimizer is a small coordinate polling routine. It evaluates the initial point, then every negative and positive coordinate displacement in a fixed order. If the best candidate reduces the minimized objective by more than 10-12, it accepts that candidate; otherwise it halves the step. The initial step is 0.4 radians. It stops when the step falls below 10-6 radians or after 128 sweeps, and records which condition stopped each run. Coordinates are wrapped modulo 2π2\pi2π.

QAOA initial points are (γ,β)=(0.2,0.1),(0.6,0.4),(−0.3,0.25)(\gamma,\beta)=(0.2,0.1),(0.6,0.4),(-0.3,0.25)(γ,β)=(0.2,0.1),(0.6,0.4),(−0.3,0.25). VQE initial angles are 0.6,−1.1,2.20.6,-1.1,2.20.6,−1.1,2.2. Every optimization objective uses an exact ideal statevector, with zero measurement shots. The run record retains accepted points, rejected candidates, iterations, evaluation counts, parameters, elapsed objective time, and total classical optimization time. Wall-clock timings include the classical simulator and are machine-dependent; they are not hardware execution times.

The primary fitted angles are then evaluated in separate ideal finite-shot experiments with seeds 7, 19, and 41, using 2,048 shots per measurement group per seed. Samples are drawn classically from statevector probabilities; repeated physical circuits are not executed. These first-increment experiments do not feed sampled objectives back to the optimizer. They report counts, sampled objectives, exact expectations, sample standard errors, and achieved output quality. Changing initialization probes optimization variability; changing the sampling seed probes readout variability. The next increment records an actual sampled parameter search with fresh final validation. Both increments use ideal simulation and exclude device noise and error mitigation.

Logical workload and the pending physical handoff¶

The logical resource record accompanies actual measured OpenQASM 3 circuits. It records gate counts, depth, width, input preparation, all sampling repetitions, basis changes, and readout. The graph is a classical edge list and the Hamiltonian is three explicit Pauli terms; no data-loading oracle is assumed. Classical input parsing, physical reset and measurement time, and device routing are excluded. Reference calculations are included in the whole CPU workflow timer, but their separate time is unmeasured.

The first demonstrated workload contains six exact classical optimization runs with their entire evaluation traces, plus three seeded readout experiments for each primary state. The ideal readout protocol represents 6,144 four-qubit QAOA preparations and 12,288 two-qubit VQE preparations, including both measurement bases. The generated ledger weights the explicit circuits by these shot counts; the samples themselves are drawn classically from statevector probabilities. Exact optimization evaluations use zero shots. The finite-shot increment provides a separate measured trace and shot allocation for every sampled training call. Multiplying one final circuit by an optimizer iteration count would not establish that workload’s convergence or accuracy.

The elementary exports retain H, CX, and one-qubit rotations. Arbitrary-angle RX, RY, and RZ gates are not synthesized into Clifford+T. The inputs support a later synthesis audit, but supply no validated T count, code distance, factory allocation, physical-qubit count, or quantum runtime. Coherent rotation approximation error and stochastic fault probability need separate budgets before a surface-code comparison. The existing Grover baseline remains a separate frozen example.

End-to-end resource analysis¶

Space/time scaling and empirical performance¶

A depth-ppp QAOA circuit for a graph with nnn vertices and mmm edges uses nnn prepared wires, pmpmpm problem rotations, pnpnpn mixer rotations and two CX gates per edge per layer before lowering. It finally measures all nnn wires. Routing and parallelism depend on the graph and hardware; an O(p(n+m))O(p(n+m))O(p(n+m)) gate count does not specify physical depth. Multiply each parameter/group circuit by its actual shots and optimizer evaluation count. The optimizer’s required evaluations and attained quality as nnn grows are empirical quantities, not fixed by this circuit formula.

VQE space follows the chosen ansatz and Hamiltonian; time also includes ansatz depth, separately measured noncommuting groups, covariance-aware estimation, shots and parameter search. The two-qubit ansatz is a bounded model. Classical exact diagonalization/exhaustive MaxCut are reference computations with independent scale limits.

The published circuit-scaling exports and changed toy instances provide bounded logical/empirical evidence. Conditional physical reports cover specified final readouts and recorded trajectories at those sizes. They are not a physical scaling law, predicted future adaptive path or general optimizer guarantee.

Verification and formalization¶

The original exact workflow has 40 dedicated checks. Additional sampled, projection, replay and second-instance checks exercise the extended workflows; each notebook is freshly executed before publication. QAOA is checked against exhaustive classical cut enumeration and independently assembled dense evolution, including asymmetric multiple-layer angles. Tests verify cost signs, angle conventions, bit ordering and complete evaluation traces.

The two-qubit VQE example is checked against the full Hamiltonian spectrum and an independent analytic energy expression. Its ansatz contains this particular toy ground state; that does not imply general VQE expressibility or convergence. All optimizer starts, rejected proposals, stopping conditions and objective calls are recorded.

Readout tests retain the covariance of jointly measured Z terms, independent X/Z streams, reproducible counts, bounded-outcome Hoeffding calculations and shot-weighted gate totals. Sample standard errors are descriptive; the stated ideal bounds are per fixed estimate, not simultaneous guarantees across every seed or optimizer parameter.

The chapter explicitly distinguishes exact-statevector optimization from ideal sampled final validation. Actual ideal finite-shot trajectories are now recorded. Conditional projections replay their fixed measured paths; a hardware/noisy optimizer trajectory, certified synthesis and formal proof remain unestablished. A separate 32-qubit generation run verifies export/counts without simulation or optimization.
