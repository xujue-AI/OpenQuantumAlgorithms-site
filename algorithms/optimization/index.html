Quantum optimization

Motivation and intuition¶

Optimization asks for a useful classical answer: a good assignment, an energy estimate, or a verified solution. For nnn binary variables there are 2n2^n2n assignments. Quantum algorithms prepare amplitudes over candidates and change them through objective-dependent phases, interference, or Hamiltonian evolution. Measurement still returns a limited classical result. Input construction, state preparation, output quality, repetitions, and classical control therefore determine whether a proposed improvement is meaningful.

This chapter checks transparent small circuits for three approaches and adds an executed fixed DQI circuit and public companion. QAOA and VQE use a classical parameter search; a digitized anneal follows a prescribed Hamiltonian path. The original algorithm papers provide the theoretical context Farhi et al., 2014Peruzzo et al., 2014Farhi et al., 2000. The implementations, exposition, and figures here are original.

Evidence and scope

The demonstrations are executed ideal numerical checks with independent classical references and downloadable circuits. Sample counts are generated classically from circuit probabilities, rather than measured on a QPU. Small results and parameterized generators do not establish large-instance convergence or quantum advantage.

Fault-tolerant reports are conditional resource models. Classical C4 reference coverage only. The reproduced Lean artifact checks finite cut values and the stated classical equality predicate; its coverage excludes QAOA and annealing correctness and physical estimates. These distinctions remain explicit below.

VQE: a variational energy and explicit measurements¶

VQE minimizes E(θ)=⟨ψ(θ)∣H∣ψ(θ)⟩E(\theta)=\langle\psi(\theta)|H|\psi(\theta)\rangleE(θ)=⟨ψ(θ)∣H∣ψ(θ)⟩. For a normalized state this is at least the ground energy, but a restricted ansatz or an unsuccessful optimizer can prevent reaching it. Estimating the expectation also requires enough repetitions of its measurement groups Peruzzo et al., 2014.

Our first input is

H=−Z0−Z1+12X0X1.H=-Z_0-Z_1+\tfrac12X_0X_1.H=−Z0​−Z1​+21​X0​X1​.
(3)

RY(theta) on qubit zero followed by CX(0,1) prepares cos⁡(θ/2)∣00⟩+sin⁡(θ/2)∣11⟩\cos(\theta/2)|00\rangle+\sin(\theta/2)|11\ranglecos(θ/2)∣00⟩+sin(θ/2)∣11⟩. Its independently calculated energy is −2cos⁡θ+12sin⁡θ-2\cos\theta+\tfrac12\sin\theta−2cosθ+21​sinθ, whose minimum matches the full matrix ground energy −17/2-\sqrt{17}/2−17​/2. This particular ansatz contains this particular ground state; it is not a general two-qubit ansatz.

One all-Z group jointly scores −Z0−Z1-Z_0-Z_1−Z0​−Z1​, retaining covariance between the terms. A separate all-X group applies H to both qubits and scores 12X0X1\tfrac12X_0X_121​X0​X1​. Adiabatic optimization: follow a path, then digitize it¶

For the same C4 MaxCut task, choose

HD=−∑jXj,HP=12∑(u,v)ZuZv=m2I−C,H_D=-\sum_jX_j,\qquad H_P=\tfrac12\sum_{(u,v)}Z_uZ_v=\tfrac m2I-C,HD​=−j∑​Xj​,HP​=21​(u,v)∑​Zu​Zv​=2m​I−C,
(4)
H(s)=(1−s)HD+sHP,s=t/T.H(s)=(1-s)H_D+sH_P,\qquad s=t/T.H(s)=(1−s)HD​+sHP​,s=t/T.
(5)

The easy driver ground state is ∣+⟩⊗4|+\rangle^{\otimes4}∣+⟩⊗4. A sufficiently suitable path and evolution time can transfer weight toward the problem ground space. Adiabatic reasoning depends on spectral and path assumptions; it gives no generic polynomial runtime guarantee for arbitrary optimization instances Farhi et al., 2000.

The implementation uses midpoint Strang slices. With Δt=T/L\Delta t=T/LΔt=T/L, each slice applies driver-half RX(-dt*(1-s)), then an explicit CX; RZ(dt*s); CX interaction per edge, then the second driver half. The source has nnn H, 2nL2nL2nL RX, mLmLmL RZ, and 2mL2mL2mL CX before final measurement.

The fixed main run is T=16,L=256T=16,L=256T=16,L=256, selected before the studies. Its ideal maximum-cut probability is approximately 0.987022729, meeting the predeclared target 0.95. Decoded quantum interferometry: optimization through decoding¶

DQI adds a recent approach that connects optimization to coding theory Jordan et al., 2025. Its quantum circuit must contain the reversible decoder as well as state preparation, syndrome computation and Fourier interference. A classical decoder runtime is not that circuit’s fault-tolerant cost. The method page distinguishes the paper’s algebraically structured result from sparse max-XORSAT experiments, where a tailored classical solver beats the showcased DQI result. The fixed nine-wire coherent circuit and ordinary public saved-artifact notebook have executed. The earlier combined attempt remains incomplete after its notebook timeout; the later notebook run checks preserved evidence without repeating the quantum science. Scalable decoder families and full physical estimates remain planned.

Verification and formalization¶

Numerical coverage includes classical enumeration and scalar expectation formulas for QAOA, dense Hamiltonian eigenvalues for VQE, gate-versus-dense Strang checks and a continuous numerical reference for annealing. Export/lowering checks compare the implemented circuits up to global phase with measurement maps retained. Finite-shot bounds apply to fresh fixed-state estimates and remain separate from optimizer success and physical faults. The size study additionally checks all graph optima by enumeration, all chain ground energies by dense diagonalization, selected-state circuits against independent references, and every measured-decision trace from its counts. Its simultaneous 84-group holdout bounds do not cover adaptive training queries or extend the classical C4 proof.
