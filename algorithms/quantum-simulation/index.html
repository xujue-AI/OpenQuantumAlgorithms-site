Quantum simulation

Motivation and intuition¶

Quantum simulation asks for a specified state transformation or an observable of a physical model. This umbrella separates three tasks because their inputs, quantum circuits, accuracy requirements and readout costs differ.

TrackTaskCurrent evidence
Hamiltonian dynamicsApproximate e−iHte^{-iHt}e−iHt or measure a stated observable after evolutionChecked product formulas, fixed two-term multiproduct circuits, word-indexed Taylor LCU, retargetable QSP and oblivious amplification; bounded logical and conditional physical studies
Open systemsImplement a specified quantum channel or Lindblad evolutionExplicit damping, one-spin splitting and interacting chains; uniform ideal-channel workloads for two, three and four spins, with wider physical studies pending
Thermal/Gibbs preparationPrepare a thermal state, its purification, or estimate a thermal observableDiagonal preparation, Pauli filtering, thermal spectral filtering and known-input amplification; bounded conditional physical reports, with general filtering planned

Each example fixes the physical model, input, units, requested output and measurement protocol. Hamiltonian dynamics uses an open noncommuting spin chain. Open-system dynamics includes absorbing decay and positive-time splitting of driven and interacting spin models. Thermal preparation separates exhaustive diagonal coefficient loading from a Bell-input filter and a reflection about that known prepared input. These are different tasks and are not an equal-task resource comparison.

The dense classical answers check actual gate/dilation/preparation circuits; they are not quantum algorithm implementations. Parameterized generators and simulators have separate limits. Classical weights for the diagonal thermal example also permit direct classical sampling, so that circuit supplies no efficiency claim.

Postselected circuits include acceptance/retry accounting. Complex QSP and word-Taylor dynamics have actual integer-round oblivious-amplification paths; the thermal filter has a separate known-input amplification circuit that preserves the accepted purification’s direction and can overrotate. Deterministic amplification, general scalable thermal filtering and certified synthesis remain planned. Channel and thermal circuits distinguish retained state output from measured trials, count or disclose preparation/reset/discard/readout, and separate approximation, coherent synthesis, statistical error and physical faults. No example supplies a completed fault-tolerant application pipeline or a claimed hardware advantage.

Fundamental methods and circuit demos¶

Use product formulas and the executed spin-chain circuit for unitary evolution; dilation and its two-wire circuit for an irreversible channel; and purification and its circuit for a thermal mixed state. Important methods have their own pages: structured word-Taylor access, QSP, oblivious amplification, one-spin Lindblad splitting, coupled-spin dynamics, noncommuting thermal filtering and known-input thermal amplification.

Further algorithm families and recent thermal methods¶

The Hamiltonian method map extends the implemented dynamics examples with checked bounded randomized and fixed two-term multi-product methods. Interaction-picture methods, optimized high-order multi-product methods and matched fault-tolerant architecture comparisons remain planned.

Recent Gibbs preparation has separate method pages. The filtered-jump detailed-balance method, following Efficient quantum thermal simulation Chen et al., 2025, now has checked Gaussian access and a restricted finite diagonal zero-C reset channel. Its finite mesh bias, 64-round numerical channel powers and eight-round readout projection have separate contracts; general correction, mixing and physical accounting remain planned. The forward evolution with a reusable bath qubit, following Simple and efficient end-to-end quantum thermal and ground state preparation Ding et al., 2026, has bounded one-spin gate and finite-channel components plus a portable saved notebook. A separate local-spin-chain companion adds finite n=1/2 checks and a source generator through n=64; its four-round outputs retain large target biases. General preparation guarantees, continuum/total accuracy and physical accounting remain planned. Mixing time, temperature, access and low-energy assumptions remain part of each task. Our diagonal loader and Bell-input filter do not implement these algorithms, and no universal efficient low-temperature preparation is claimed.

End-to-end resource analysis¶

Scaling parameters¶

Hamiltonian dynamics depends on system size, term structure, time and accuracy. Open-system work adds jump operators, channel discretization and fresh environment cost. Thermal preparation adds inverse temperature, normalization and accepted-output probability. The diagonal thermal loader explicitly takes exponential classical work; the tiny noncommuting filter is bounded. Each track keeps its own space/time ledger and simulator limits. Their toy outputs do not establish one common size-dependent physical frontier.

The Hamiltonian-dynamics report contains logical workloads and bounded conditional physical studies for product formulas and QSP. Structured Taylor and interacting-channel pages also provide prescribed size/time/accuracy workloads. The retargetable-QSP and thermal-spectral pages include actual Clifford+T measured circuits and complete-attempt physical ledgers for one and two spins. All three tracks retain explicit limits; larger physical studies remain pending where reset, conditioning or synthesis contracts are unsupported.

TrackRequired output contractCosts that must remain explicit
Hamiltonian dynamicsUnitary/state evolution or a measured observable at a stated timePreparation, access, approximation, synthesis, postselection/amplification, measurements and repetitions
Open systemsA specified channel/state or observableEnvironment/dilation preparation, reset/discard, channel approximation and measurement
Gibbs preparationA density operator, purification or thermal observableTemperature, Hamiltonian access, normalization/overlap assumptions, preparation and success costs

These are different tasks. A cosine filter is not full complex evolution, and a classical matrix exponential is a reference rather than a quantum preparation algorithm. Align the task and accuracy before comparing costs.

Use separate approximation, sampling and physical-fault budgets. The static arbitrary-rotation adapter supplies conditional mean-fit projections. A separate serialized instrument model charges reset, readout, controller, idle and factory work for its checked bounded circuits. Actual synthesis sequences in that model still rely on numerical guards and stated routing/layout premises; they do not certify hardware confidence. The fault-tolerance appendix explains these boundaries.

Verification and formalization¶

Hamiltonian-dynamics verification records the available circuit, operator/state, postselection and polynomial checks. Its evidence includes complex QSP and probabilistic amplification; certified physical synthesis, deterministic amplification and a formal proof remain absent.

Open-system circuits are compared with independent Kraus, Bloch-equation and Liouvillian references. Checks preserve the selected input, rates, time, observable and error metric, and distinguish a complete channel from a selected measurement branch. Additional Choi checks test complete positivity and trace preservation on the bounded models.

Thermal preparation compares actual prepared states and observables with normalized matrix-exponential targets at the same Hamiltonian and temperature. Classical preprocessing, normalization, acceptance and purification assumptions remain visible. General many-body channel access and scalable Gibbs preparation remain planned. No reproduced Lean formalization or synthesis certificate is included.
