Ripple-carry modular arithmetic

The factoring circuit needs reversible modular multiplication. Fourier arithmetic stores addition in phases; ripple arithmetic carries ordinary binary information along a register using reversible Boolean gates. This variant makes the tradeoff visible: more work qubits and compiled operations, but many fewer general-angle rotations in the demonstrated circuits.

Implementation status

The carry, modular-add and modular-multiply circuits are implemented and numerically checked. The full N=15N=15N=15 and N=21N=21N=21 circuits are simulated with a bounded complex-amplitude source simulator. Small coherent circuits are independently checked with Qiskit. Larger generators and their logical counts are separate from quantum simulation. Physical results below are an equal-task conditional QDK model comparison, not an observed hardware speedup or a synthesis certificate.

Fundamental carry construction¶

Our original implementation uses the MAJ/UMA idea of Cuccaro, Draper, Kutin and Moulton Cuccaro et al., 2004. For bit wires (a,b,c)(a,b,c)(a,b,c), MAJ applies CX from aaa to bbb, CX from aaa to ccc, and CCX from (b,c)(b,c)(b,c) to aaa. The final aaa stores the majority/carry. Reverse UMA restores aaa and ccc while replacing bbb with their sum bit. Chaining MAJ from low to high bits and UMA in reverse gives

∣a,b,c⟩⟼∣a,(a+b+c) mod 2m,c⟩.|a,b,c\rangle\longmapsto |a,(a+b+c)\bmod 2^m,c\rangle.∣a,b,c⟩⟼∣a,(a+b+c)mod2m,c⟩.
(1)

The circuit exposes no overflow output. It uses 2m+12m+12m+1 wires, 2m2m2m Toffoli gates and 4m4m4m CX gates, with depth linear in mmm. These intentionally simple endpoints are not the paper’s optimized gate count.

From addition to a clean modular multiplier¶

Let n=⌈log⁡2N⌉n=\lceil\log_2N\rceiln=⌈log2​N⌉ and m=n+1m=n+1m=n+1. A constant addition prepares the constant into an mmm-bit zero register, runs the carry adder, then unprepares the restored constant register. Preparation uses X, CX or CCX for zero, one or two external controls. The adder itself remains an explicitly defined Boolean circuit.

A modular addition first adds bbb, subtracts NNN, copies the accumulator’s high bit into a borrow flag, and conditionally restores NNN. Subtracting bbb reveals the complementary sign condition needed to erase the flag; adding bbb restores the final result. The carry and constant register are clean after every addition. The subtract/restore work also cancels on an inactive control branch.

To multiply, add (a2j mod N)(a2^j\bmod N)(a2jmodN) for each input bit into a zero accumulator, swap the input/result under the external control, then subtract the inverse multiplier’s weighted constants to erase the old input. The circuit includes every control, constant preparation, swap and uncomputation. No dense unitary or arithmetic lookup table constructs a gate.

The valid contract is x<Nx<Nx<N, zero accumulator, borrow, constant and carry workspace. The same reversible circuit has an unspecified extension on invalid or dirty inputs. In-memory gates preserve the relative phases needed for coherent controls. Fully measured QASM exports omit the compiled overall global phase, recorded in their ledger; they are not phase-preserving specifications for another controlled oracle.

End-to-end space and time scaling¶

Constant addition has O(n)O(n)O(n) gates and depth. Five additions plus borrow handling implement one modular addition; 2n2n2n modular additions and nnn controlled swaps implement one nonidentity multiplier. Consequently the complete coherent circuit has

Qripple=t+3n+4,Gripple=O(tn2+t2).Q_{\rm ripple}=t+3n+4,\qquad G_{\rm ripple}=O(tn^2+t^2).Qripple​=t+3n+4,Gripple​=O(tn2+t2).
(2)

At t=2nt=2nt=2n, this is O(n3)O(n^3)O(n3) logical gate growth, compared with the implemented exact-Fourier baseline’s O(n4)O(n^4)O(n4), at the cost of n+2n+2n+2 extra wires. This asymptotic comparison does not make the small ripple circuits cheaper in every metric. Classical simulation and verification¶

The Boolean simulator interprets the actual X/CX/CCX/SWAP/CSWAP source gates using integer bit operations. It checks active and inactive controls and clean work registers without allocating an exponentially large statevector. Basis checks alone are distinguished from coherent checks: tests also compare Qiskit complex amplitudes on superpositions, including relative phase, and compare a small lowered circuit with its original statevector.

The sparse source simulator applies the actual H, phase and Boolean gates to complex amplitudes without truncation. N=15N=15N=15 and N=21N=21N=21 probability discrepancies from the independent order/Fourier reference are about 3.33×10−163.33\times10^{-16}3.33×10−16 and 2.91×10−162.91\times10^{-16}2.91×10−16; dirty-work probability is zero. N21 uses at most 380 nonzero amplitudes and 818,384 gate-state applications, rather than a dense 25-wire allocation. The independent reviewer also compared this simulator with Qiskit on general small phase/Boolean circuits.

The 36 dedicated tests cover carries, controls, modular inverses, clean ancillas, coherent relative phase, source/compiled/sparse checks and limits. An independent interpreter additionally checked 43,688 carry-adder cases, 13,426 modular-add branches and 2,280 multiplier branches through N=33N=33N=33, plus inverse cases. These are scoped computational checks, not a machine-checked theorem for every width. No Lean proof is integrated for this variant.

Reproduction, interaction and limits¶

The executed notebook reuses the implementation and provides optional live Python controls for bounded N15/N21 cases. Static publication omits those kernel controls; a local or Binder kernel can regenerate the actual circuits and plots. The documentation itself remains static.

python scripts/generate_shor_ripple.py --physical
pytest tests/test_shor_ripple.py
python scripts/generate_shor_ripple.py --modulus 33 --base 2 --phase-bits 12 \
  --no-sim --no-extra --output-dir artifacts/ripple33

Input generation permits at most 12 integer bits and 24 exponent bits, with an arithmetic-work preflight and 500,000 compiled-operation cap. Source lowering is capped at 64 wires and 100,000 primitives. The quantum sparse simulator separately caps exponent precision at eight bits, support at 8,192 amplitudes and work at four million gate-state applications. Dense Qiskit checks cap both 20 wires and a separate gate-amplitude work estimate; a full N15 dense check is optional and took about 34 seconds in the recorded CPU environment. These caps reject costly requests instead of promising simulation from a circuit’s logical width.

The generator’s --no-sim skips quantum amplitude simulation; bounded Boolean checks remain. For N≤33N\le33N≤33 these cover every valid input/control branch; larger generated multipliers check four named input values only. Large circuit materialization is not a validated large factoring run.
