Fault-tolerant resources: order finding

Planned analysis

No Shor circuit resource report is published here. This page specifies what must be included before a factoring estimate can be reviewed. See the implementation plan and verification protocol.

Define the complete factoring task¶

Record the integer NNN, its bit width, the permitted base-selection procedure, the required probability of obtaining a nontrivial factor, and the arithmetic design. Order finding for one fixed base is a subroutine; its cost alone does not cover unsuccessful base choices, unsuccessful phase samples, or factor reconstruction. Shor’s original paper supplies the algorithmic reference.

Build a workload ledger¶

StageWork to materialize and count
Classical preparationInput validation, special-case handling, base selection, and coprimality checks
Quantum preparationControl and work registers, initialization, and clean arithmetic ancillas
Controlled arithmeticEvery required modular multiplication, carry workspace, reversals, and uncomputation
Phase readoutInverse-QFT or adaptive readout circuit, approximation cutoff, and final measurements
Repeated attemptsThe declared sampling/base-choice procedure and its success accounting
Classical outputRational reconstruction, candidate-order checks, greatest common divisors, and factor validation

An arithmetic box named “modular exponentiation” is not a primitive-gate resource count. The generator must expose its reversible implementation. Report both bit-width scaling and concrete decomposed small instances; a circuit simplified using a known order or factor needs a separate pedagogical label.

Approximation and physical mapping¶

Allocate phase-estimation precision, inverse-QFT approximation, and coherent rotation synthesis before mapping the circuit to error correction. Keep approximation allowances separate from the probabilities of sampling failure and physical faults. The current shared estimator supports bound static circuits with final measurements; adaptive phase estimation needs a branch-aware extension.

Use the fault-tolerance appendix to specify the code, decoder, physical errors, operation times, factory choices, and scheduling model. The frozen Grover surface-code report illustrates the reporting format; its numerical result is not a bound for factoring.

Arbitrary-angle costs from the shared synthesis helper are conditional mean-fit projections. They do not supply a concrete synthesized circuit, an instance approximation certificate, or a worst-case runtime guarantee. A future comparison must hold the factoring task and its accuracy/success requirements fixed while changing the implementation. No quantum-advantage claim follows from the planned resource ledger.
