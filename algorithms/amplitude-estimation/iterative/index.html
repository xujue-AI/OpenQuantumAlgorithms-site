Adaptive amplitude estimation

Adaptive amplitude estimation uses repeated amplified flag measurements to narrow a probability interval. Each quantum trial uses only data and flag qubits; a classical controller chooses the next amplification power from the preceding interval. Grinko and colleagues established iterative amplitude estimation as a distinct approach Grinko et al., 2021; the bounded fresh-batch variant here specifies its own sampling and stopping rules.

Implemented and checked scope

Explicit threshold circuits, their independent amplified-flag laws, seeded sampled trajectories, conservative inference arithmetic, and complete recorded-path accounting are implemented and checked. The default reaches its requested interval width after four fresh batches. A separate 10-case circuit suite and 224-run model-only coverage diagnostic retain their full records. Resource projections remain conditional on the recorded ideal path; no hardware confidence certificate or quantum advantage is established.

Task, input and intuition¶

Estimate a=k/2na=k/2^na=k/2n for the explicit uniform threshold preparation, with requested absolute error ϵ\epsilonϵ and ideal statistical failure allowance δ\deltaδ. The threshold has an exact classical shortcut, so it is a circuit and inference example rather than an application benchmark.

Use the normalized angle t=arcsin⁡(a)/π∈[0,1/2]t=\arcsin(\sqrt a)/\pi\in[0,1/2]t=arcsin(a​)/π∈[0,1/2], so a=sin⁡2(πt)a=\sin^2(\pi t)a=sin2(πt). After preparing A∣0⟩A|0\rangleA∣0⟩ and applying the actual Grover iterate QQQ rrr times, the flag probability is

pr=sin⁡2(π(2r+1)t).p_r=\sin^2(\pi(2r+1)t).pr​=sin2(π(2r+1)t).
(1)

Larger multipliers magnify an angle change, but also introduce aliases: several angles can have the same amplified probability. The controller maintains a valid angle interval and chooses the largest permitted odd multiplier whose entire unwrapped interval lies on one lobe [j/2,(j+1)/2][j/2,(j+1)/2][j/2,(j+1)/2] of sin⁡2(πu)\sin^2(\pi u)sin2(πu). Exact rational comparisons establish this monotone branch. The known threshold answer checks the example, rather than choosing powers or stopping inference.

Explicit circuits and sampled rounds¶

Every trial is a fresh A∣0⟩A|0\rangleA∣0⟩ preparation, followed by QrQ^rQr and a computational-basis measurement of the flag. Preparation, inverse preparation and both reflections occur inside each actual iterate. Data wires are discarded; measuring only the flag is the requested scalar output. All powers are repeated actual gates, without a dense unitary or probability oracle standing in for the quantum circuit.

For each new round, the controller selects its power from the preceding interval, runs exactly one fresh fixed-size batch, and computes a two-sided Clopper–Pearson interval for the amplified flag probability. The round receives δj=δ/T\delta_j=\delta/Tδj​=δ/T, where TTT is the declared maximum number of rounds. It inverts the interval on the previously chosen branch and intersects the resulting angle interval with the prior one. Counts from different rounds are not pooled or reused as a fixed-size confidence interval after optional stopping.

The algorithm reports a conclusive midpoint only when its certified radius about the actual reported float is at most ϵ\epsilonϵ, which also ensures interval width at most 2ϵ2\epsilon2ϵ. A power/round/shot cap can instead return inconclusive; an empty intersection is also retained explicitly. The program never quietly changes the requested accuracy or reports a capped wide interval as a successful estimate.

Ideal confidence and variants¶

Conditional on every preceding history, the new fixed-size batch has binomial law with its predictably chosen power. Its Clopper–Pearson miss probability is at most δ/T\delta/Tδ/T. A union bound therefore bounds the probability of any miss by δ\deltaδ over the capped run. On the event that every interval covers, monotone inversion and intersection preserve the true angle. A conclusive midpoint then has error at most ϵ\epsilonϵ.

The resulting ideal statement is P(conclusive and wrong)≤δP(\text{conclusive and wrong})\le\deltaP(conclusive and wrong)≤δ. It is not a claim that P(correct∣conclusive)≥1−δP(\text{correct}\mid\text{conclusive})\ge1-\deltaP(correct∣conclusive)≥1−δ, and caps do not guarantee convergence.

SciPy beta quantiles seed the interval endpoints, which move outward until exact integer/rational binomial-tail inequalities verify the allocated error. The per-batch allocation is rounded down from δ/T\delta/Tδ/T. Probability inversion uses conservative 192-bit fixed-point sine-squared enclosures, rational Machin bounds for π\piπ, and a bounded Taylor remainder; angle conversion and the radius around the reported floating midpoint are outward bounded. These are executed conservative arithmetic certificates, separate from a Lean proof or a certified iid PRNG/device sampler.

End-to-end resource analysis¶

For a recorded trial at power rrr, the circuit uses n+1n+1n+1 live qubits, one initial AAA, rrr additional AAA, rrr A†A^\daggerA†, both reflections rrr times and one flag measurement. It therefore evaluates the threshold predicate or its inverse 1+2r1+2r1+2r times. Each selected shot pays that entire schedule, including the rounds that do not narrow the interval enough to stop.

The logical ledger weights actual lowered circuits by every recorded shot, preserving per-power OpenQASM hashes, gate counts, dependency depth and all access/preparation/readout calls. The trajectory records all selected powers, confidence/branch decisions, counts, intervals, seeds and RNG provenance. Classical control and compilation time are distinct from modeled quantum runtime.

The default has three live qubits and powers [0,0,0,2][0,0,0,2][0,0,0,2]. The three power-zero batches have 768 shots; the power-two batch has 256. The complete workload contains 512 QQQ calls, 2,048 threshold/inverse calls and 1,024 flag measurements:

Here each CCC denotes the compiled cost of the supplied preparation, inverse, zero/good reflection or flag readout, including their actual comparator controls. The analogous serial dependency-depth sum is recorded separately; synthesis, code distance and factory scheduling change physical time and space. No CAC_ACA​ or reflection is treated as a unit-cost oracle.

The declared caps supply the crude source-access bound Nmax⁡(1+2rmax⁡)N_{\max}(1+2r_{\max})Nmax​(1+2rmax​), with no promise that the caps achieve precision. There is no empirical asymptotic quantum-runtime claim for this particular capped variant. Qubit width is n+1n+1n+1; primitive cost depends on the implemented threshold comparator, reflections, power and pinned decomposition.

The optional physical report estimates each unique full measured trial circuit with the same illustrative surface-code profile. A whole-recorded-path physical-fault allowance 0.005 is divided over all actual shots, so every execution receives 0.005/N0.005/N0.005/N. A separate per-output synthesis-model allowance 10-5 remains an approximation-model scalar rather than a random fault probability.

A serial envelope combines the returned finite per-power Pareto frontiers: peak qubits are the maximum selected allocation and time is the sum of complete per-power trial times weighted by shots. Grouping is accounting only; the recorded adaptive round order is preserved. It assumes factory/layout reconfiguration between groups with unmeasured latency; initialization/reset, classical decision, decoding and compilation times are excluded. It retains the per-group code distances and factory allocations rather than adding logical widths. Every projected circuit’s QASM hash is checked against its exported complete measured trial, and the resource adapter is included in the source hashes and snapshots.

Verification and formalization¶

The generator’s 10 predetermined threshold cases include exact endpoints, quarter/half examples and neighboring five-bit thresholds. All 10 selected runs reach conclusive intervals and use 7,680 ideal trials in total. Their actual bounded circuit probabilities agree with the independent amplified law within 2×10−142\times10^{-14}2×10−14. This checks the selected seeds, not guaranteed convergence for every seed or permitted instance.

A separate 224-run sweep covers seven amplitudes with 32 predetermined seeds each, using the same inference engine with an explicitly model-only analytic flag law. It models 111,104 shots; all selected runs are conclusive, with zero observed interval misses or conclusive errors above target. These frequencies are diagnostics, not a probability certificate or physical circuit experiment. Full seed/status/interval records distinguish them from the circuit suite.

The executed notebook shows the circuit, every default sampled round and the weighted resource ledger. Static publication reuses saved outputs and needs no public kernel backend. Optional local/Binder controls regenerate bounded circuits and trajectories. Executed arithmetic certificates cover endpoint/alias/inversion/radius calculations; no Lean formalization, verified compiler, iid sampler proof or hardware confidence proof is integrated.

Circuit construction can also run independently of adaptive statevector inference:

compiled = lower_circuit(trial)

This bounded seven-wire export was generated and counted at 15,937 primitive operations without statevector validation. Its size is not a claim that every allowed larger input is affordable.

The generator supports explicit accuracy/shot/round/power caps. Circuit generation supports power up to 4,096 with a 250,000 source-instruction guard that counts MCX atomically. Bounded statevector inference supports at most eight data wires and power 128; hitting that bound returns its explicit simulation-cap status. Primitive export is separately capped at 250,000 after lowering, which can consume extra compilation work before the guard. The generator removes stale optional reports and old per-power exports, uses a temporary factory cache outside public assets, records environment/source/lock/RNG hashes and runs without a Git directory in the public code package.

The original implementation uses Qiskit core 2.6.2 (Apache-2.0) for explicit gates, exports and ideal statevectors Qiskit contributors, n.d., SciPy 1.18.1 for initial binomial-interval quantile guesses SciPy developers, n.d., NumPy 2.5.4 for seeded counts and Matplotlib 3.11.2 for original figures.
