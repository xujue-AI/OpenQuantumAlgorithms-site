Quantum linear systems

Motivation and intuition¶

Linear systems occur in differential equations, regression and optimization. A quantum linear-system algorithm prepares a state proportional to A−1bA^{-1}bA−1b and extracts a useful observable. This can be attractive when a matrix has economical quantum access and the downstream task needs a few expectations rather than the entire vector. Loading an arbitrary matrix or reading every solution entry can remove that attraction. The survey’s end-to-end perspective makes access and output assumptions central Dalzell et al., 2025.

The intuition is spectral: in an eigenbasis of AAA, divide each amplitude of bbb by its eigenvalue. HHL stores the eigenvalue in a clock, uses it to control a flag rotation, then erases the clock Harrow et al., 2009. QSVT instead applies a polynomial to a block encoding of AAA, avoiding the eigenvalue register Gilyén et al., 2019. Both need normalization, a success procedure and readout.

Implemented scope

HHL is implemented for a dyadic commuting matrix family, alongside a spectrum-matched QSVT inverse. Small circuits are checked against independent classical solves; larger structured circuits have separate generation limits. These families are also easy to solve classically. General sparse-matrix HHL, arbitrary vector loading, a general high-accuracy QSVT reciprocal, amplitude amplification and integrated formal proofs remain further implementations. Surface-code outputs are conditional cost projections with excluded initialization and uncertified synthesis.

Synthesis and initialization limits apply prominently. QDK’s PSSPC rotation cost is a mean fit for random angles; it supplies no concrete synthesis sequence or operator-error certificate. Its scalar cannot certify postselected state accuracy or acceptance. Clean-state initialization timing/faults, classical coefficient/phase/lookup construction, compilation, reference solving and readout processing time are excluded. QDK abstracts Clifford scheduling; dependency depth is not a physical schedule.

QASM exports describe complete measured trials. The pinned exporter can omit an overall compiled global phase; ledgers record it. These exports are not phase-preserving coherently controlled oracle specifications.

Verification and formalization¶

HHL tests compare QFT with an independently constructed Fourier matrix, controlled evolution with matrix exponentials including identity phase, eigeninputs with expected clock labels, reciprocal flag probabilities with their formula, and complete states with NumPy solves. Coverage includes all-ones clock labels, spectators, product inputs, invalid parameters, lowering equivalence and actual X/Y/Z readout. Independent review also checked 30 random Kronecker-X systems and 33 boundary cases.

QSVT tests compare accepted blocks with an independently computed cubic, cover Y-containing words, verify preparation and measurement, reproduce numerical phases, and include a generic-spectrum case that correctly does not claim inversion. Its phase evidence separates polynomial algebra from numerical coefficient/grid checks.

No Lean formalization is integrated. External proof libraries do not verify this Python generator without an explicit theorem/circuit connection and reproduced toolchain. Numerical simulation uses bounded Qiskit Statevector and independent NumPy/SciPy references.
