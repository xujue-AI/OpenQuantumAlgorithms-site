Neumann-series linear inverse

The spectrum-matched QSVT cubic solves a special two-eigenvalue example. A broader inverse needs an approximation valid across a promised spectral interval. This page implements a finite Neumann-series inverse with Pauli LCU. It is a distinct method from a general QSVT reciprocal compiler.

The intuition is classical and transparent: if C=I−BC=I-BC=I−B is a contraction, repeated powers of CCC form a convergent geometric series for B−1B^{-1}B−1. We compute the finite polynomial’s Pauli coefficients, construct its actual PREPARE/SELECT/unprepare circuit, and measure a property of the accepted solution state. LCU supplies a quantum implementation of a linear combination; related quantum linear-system algorithms use other reciprocal expansions Berry et al., 2015Childs et al., 2017. Our finite Neumann choice, exact coefficient aggregation and example are original.

Implemented scope

A positive identity-dominant Hermitian Pauli matrix, a complex input circuit, a finite reciprocal polynomial, explicit access and ordinary known-input amplification are implemented. The analytic polynomial error is uniform across a specified interval. Tiny circuits are independently checked; larger generated circuits are marked separately. Classical Pauli aggregation is capped and can grow exponentially. General sparse matrix loading, a QSVT reciprocal phase compiler, certified hardware synthesis and Lean formalization remain unavailable.

The coefficient dominance promise ensures spec⁡(B)⊆[δ,1]\operatorname{spec}(B)\subseteq[\delta,1]spec(B)⊆[δ,1] and condition number at most 1/δ1/\delta1/δ. The source accepts exact decimal or rational coefficients; no eigenvectors, inverse matrix or solution state constructs a gate.

A=.75I−.1 IX−.1 ZI−.05 XX.A=.75I-.1\,IX-.1\,ZI-.05\,XX.A=.75I−.1IX−.1ZI−.05XX.

Qiskit’s rightmost Pauli acts on q0. This matrix is noncommuting and has four distinct eigenvalues, approximately .53820, .73820, .76180 and .96180. Its promised gap is δ=.5\delta=.5δ=.5, while its actual condition number is about 1.787. The input uses RY(.63), RZ(.41) on q0 and RY(.37) on q1, so the prepared amplitudes are complex and unbalanced.

The output task is one conditional Z(q0)Z(q_0)Z(q0​) expectation of the normalized solution A−1∣b⟩/∥A−1b∥A^{-1}|b\rangle/\|A^{-1}b\|A−1∣b⟩/∥A−1b∥. A quantum state does not return every solution entry or the overall solution norm. The tiny classical solve provides an independent reference to this same normalized output.

Uniform reciprocal approximation¶

Use the scaled polynomial

TK(B)=δ∑k=0K(I−B)k=δB−1[I−(I−B)K+1].T_K(B)=\delta\sum_{k=0}^K(I-B)^k =\delta B^{-1}\bigl[I-(I-B)^{K+1}\bigr].TK​(B)=δk=0∑K​(I−B)k=δB−1[I−(I−B)K+1].
(3)

With γ=1−δ\gamma=1-\deltaγ=1−δ and E=γK+1E=\gamma^{K+1}E=γK+1, the spectral promise gives

∥TK(B)−δB−1∥≤E.\|T_K(B)-\delta B^{-1}\|\le E.∥TK​(B)−δB−1∥≤E.
(4)

This is an analytic bound across the whole promised interval, rather than agreement only at chosen eigenvalues. Our exact rational Pauli arithmetic preserves the finite polynomial before its coefficients are converted into numerical gate angles.

Explicit polynomial access¶

The generator multiplies Pauli words using exact rational coefficients. Since each accumulated power is a polynomial in CCC, multiplication can use (CP+PC)/2(CP+PC)/2(CP+PC)/2; anticommuting pairs cancel algebraically. No tolerance-based term pruning is used.

Let TK=∑ℓtℓPℓT_K=\sum_\ell t_\ell P_\ellTK​=∑ℓ​tℓ​Pℓ​ and s=∑ℓ∣tℓ∣s=\sum_\ell|t_\ell|s=∑ℓ​∣tℓ​∣. The actual circuit prepares label amplitudes ∣tℓ∣/s\sqrt{|t_\ell|/s}∣tℓ​∣/s​, applies the signed Pauli word selected by that label, and reverses PREPARE. All-zero labels select TK/sT_K/sTK​/s. The exact coefficient bound is

δ≤s≤1−E.\delta\le s\le1-E.δ≤s≤1−E.
(6)

Because TKT_KTK​ has eigenvalues at least δ\deltaδ, unamplified success is at least (δ/s)2(\delta/s)^2(δ/s)2. The tiny numerical default acceptance is about .80094. All label and data wires are measured at the end, including rejected trials.

Known-input amplitude amplification¶

A nonunitary inverse must not be amplified by assuming it is a scaled unitary. Here WWW includes the actual input preparation and LCU. Define a good reflection Sg=I−2Πlabels=0S_g=I-2\Pi_{\rm labels=0}Sg​=I−2Πlabels=0​ and an initial reflection S0=I−2∣0⋯0⟩⟨0⋯0∣S_0=I-2|0\cdots0\rangle\langle0\cdots0|S0​=I−2∣0⋯0⟩⟨0⋯0∣ on every initial wire. Both are explicit X/MCZ/X circuits.

After WWW, one chronological round applies Sg,W†,S0,WS_g,W^\dagger,S_0,WSg​,W†,S0​,W and the global minus sign. This is ordinary amplitude amplification of the known prepared state Brassard et al., 2002. Its accepted direction remains the finite-polynomial solution in the ideal model; it is not oblivious amplification of an arbitrary inverse operator.

For known input success ppp, rrr rounds give sin⁡2((2r+1)arcsin⁡p)\sin^2((2r+1)\arcsin\sqrt p)sin2((2r+1)arcsinp​) and require 2r+12r+12r+1 complete forward/inverse source calls plus 2r2r2r reflections. Success is nonmonotone: a first round can overshoot. The notebook checks actual circuits against this formula and compares the accepted state before and after amplification.

Space, time and accuracy scaling¶

An unscaled reciprocal error bound is E/δE/\deltaE/δ. For a requested conditional Pauli-bias allowance ϵA\epsilon_AϵA​, selecting

E≤2ϵA2+ϵAE\le\frac{2\epsilon_A}{2+\epsilon_A}E≤2+ϵA​2ϵA​​
(7)

gives degree K=O(δ−1log⁡(1/ϵA))K=O(\delta^{-1}\log(1/\epsilon_A))K=O(δ−1log(1/ϵA​)) for small gap. The module computes this degree and verifies the inequality using exact rational arithmetic. A request exceeding the degree-64 cap is rejected.

The circuit uses n+⌈log⁡2M⌉n+\lceil\log_2M\rceiln+⌈log2​M⌉ logical wires, with MMM nonzero aggregated polynomial terms. Explicit SELECT work depends on the Pauli word widths, while label PREPARE has controlled rotations. Each full amplified source must be charged. If LLL is the complement input-term count and Mmax⁡M_{\max}Mmax​ is the largest intermediate power term count, aggregation can require O(KLMmax⁡)O(K L M_{\max})O(KLMmax​) word products before this circuit exists. Each product manipulates nnn-letter strings and growing rational coefficients. Final MMM and intermediate Mmax⁡M_{\max}Mmax​ can grow exponentially; the generator rejects more than 256 aggregate terms. Reducing the polynomial to a small Pauli list is classical preprocessing, rather than a free quantum oracle.

The width CSV records actual circuits at n=2,3,4,6,8,12,24n=2,3,4,6,8,12,24n=2,3,4,6,8,12,24 using three specified words: X on q0, Z on q(n−1), and X on every wire. This fixed algebra can be classically tractable, so its width scaling establishes circuit-generation behavior without establishing a general advantage. The condition CSV changes the promised gap and selects degree at fixed bias allowance. The degree CSV compares actual small-instance errors with the uniform bound.

Generation allows 24 data wires, 32 input terms, degree 64, 256 aggregate terms and 200,000 expanded primitive operations. Input rational numerators/denominators are capped at 512 bits, their normalized common denominator at 128 bits, literals at 192 characters and scientific exponents at magnitude 128. Reference solves allow six data wires and statevectors at most 12 total wires. Named large rows are circuit-only exports. The separate bounded physical study below covers selected size/gap cases; the larger logical grids remain unprojected.

Verification and formalization¶

Tests and independent reviews compare rational polynomial coefficients with separately assembled matrix powers, actual access blocks with the polynomial, and normalized accepted states with classical solves. They vary degree, conditioning, word width and complex inputs. Actual reflected circuits are checked against the amplitude formula and the unamplified accepted direction. X/Y/Z basis readout and invalid/oversized input rejection are covered. No Lean formalization or integrated synthesis theorem covers these gates.

The executed notebook reuses the implementation and its outputs; optional live controls vary a bounded two-qubit example. Live Python needs a kernel, while the saved book is static. The public code package provides shared Pauli/preparation helpers and tests; a module snapshot alone does not include its dependencies.
