Motivation and literature context¶

Forward interaction with a qubit bath offers a small environment register: choose a system coupling and bath frequency, prepare the bath, evolve the joint system, then discard and reset the bath. Ding, Zhan, Preskill and Lin analyze this approach for thermal and ground-state preparation Ding et al., 2026. Here the coupling is an actual local X operator on a sampled site, and the source expands every forward operation into visible gates. Increasing the system size changes the Hamiltonian and the emitted register and gates.

The paper’s end-to-end guarantees depend on its access normalization, temperature or spectral-gap assumptions and mixing conditions. Its stated thermal mixing examples include noninteracting spins and quadratic fermions at β=Θ(1)\beta=\Theta(1)β=Θ(1), and a separate result for local commuting systems at high temperature Ding et al., 2026. The diagonal chain below is local and commuting, but that class membership does not establish the paper’s normalization, high-temperature or mixing hypotheses for this particular pulse and sampler. No theorem is reproduced here. The finite channel may retain bias relative to the Gibbs or ground target, even when its circuit implementation agrees with the independent reference.

Scope of the cost

Rotation synthesis, T count/depth, routing, scheduling, idle exposure, controller/reset/readout timing and noise, sampling confidence, factories, code distance, physical qubits/runtime and total physical success are uncomputed. The ideal Hamiltonian duration 2K2K2K is not a physical runtime. No fault-tolerant advantage or accuracy claim follows from the primitive count.
