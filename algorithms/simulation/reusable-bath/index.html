Thermal and ground states from a reusable bath qubit

Implementation status: bounded component evidence

An original one-spin implementation has authenticated results from 165 selected tests, two complete source exports, 17 compact SDK/manual gate comparisons, 81 distinct-frequency ODE solves and six reduced channels. These components completed during an overall failed validation. Neither complete exported preparation was quantum simulated.

On 13 November 2026, a subsequent ordinary local notebook replay completed all five code cells with exit code 0. That replay authenticated and displayed saved files; it performed no new scientific computation and does not turn the earlier full validation into a pass. The saved records retain all_components_verified=false.

The general algorithm, a generator for growing system size, full fault-tolerant accounting, a continuum bridge and a reproduced proof remain planned. The requested absolute-Z error of 0.05 is uncertified.

Motivation and intuition¶

The separate local-spin-chain companion supplies n=1/2 finite-channel checks and generation-only sources through n=64. Its short four-round examples retain large target biases and no total Gibbs/ground-state accuracy or physical certificate. The parameters and evidence below remain this one-spin study’s own records.

Ding, Zhan, Preskill and Lin study thermal and ground-state preparation by sampling a coupling operator and bath frequency, applying forward system–bath evolution, discarding the bath and resetting one reusable qubit Ding et al., 2026. Repeated interaction lets the system exchange energy with fresh bath states. The desired output is the reduced system state averaged over the random choices and unobserved bath discards. A recorded choice sequence represents one preparation schedule; it is not a purification of the averaged output.

This page implements a finite one-spin prototype of that idea. It has no accepted/rejected branch or bath postselection. The thermal-filter examples use a different preparation procedure, and the detailed-balance method has separately checked Gaussian access and a restricted finite zero-C channel. Its general correction and mixing remain planned.

Fundamental method and theorem context¶

The paper proves end-to-end polynomial dependence on mixing time, inverse accuracy and temperature, or inverse Hamiltonian gap for ground-state preparation. For its stated thermal mixing guarantees, the explicit noninteracting-spin and quadratic-fermion classes use β=Θ(1)\beta=\Theta(1)β=Θ(1); a separate guarantee covers local commuting systems at high temperature Ding et al., 2026. These are literature conditions. The finite one-spin checks below retain their own channel, numerical-proxy and uncertified total-error scope; the general continuum-to-circuit bridge remains planned.

End-to-end resources and fault tolerance¶

The two emitted full source schedules include initialization, every forward gate, optional bath X, every bath reset and final system measurement. They contain one system qubit, one bath qubit and one output bit. Each complete preparation has 194 resets: one system reset and 193 bath resets, including initialization and all 192 round-end discards. Readout is one system measurement; there is no environment measurement or postselection.

Thermal preparation adds between zero and K X gates. A repeated Z trial reruns the entire preparation. M frequency nodes affect classical coefficient/law storage and reference work; they do not multiply the gates in a sampled round. Scalar K/L/M/P counting is separate from bounded source emission and does not generate coefficients, circuits or reference jobs. This is one-spin parameter counting, not a generator for large n.

The saved statistical workload is 29,958 complete preparations per mode, 59,916 readouts total, with an all-mode upper bound of 32,412,339,108 instructions. It is an unexecuted projection, not a measured sampling population. The 128 recorded schedules per mode are classical schedule audits. They are not 128 prepared quantum states.

Clifford+T synthesis, T count/depth, routing, reset/controller noise and timing, idle exposure, factories, code distance, physical qubits/runtime and total physical success remain uncomputed. The primitive totals therefore do not establish a fault-tolerant cost or superiority. Any future co-design comparison must use the same task, accuracy, success and physical assumptions; the frozen surface-code baseline is unchanged.

Verification, formalization and implementation sources¶

The retained 165 selected tests comprise 142 source/runner tests and 23 reference tests. Their completion, source exports and finite numerical checks are authenticated component evidence. The overall validation failed during notebook artifact generation; the later successful saved-file replay establishes notebook usability only. A reproduced mechanized proof, general-system implementation and exact continuum-to-circuit bridge remain planned.

The motivating paper is cited as external algorithmic evidence Ding et al., 2026; this restricted prototype does not reproduce its general theorem.
