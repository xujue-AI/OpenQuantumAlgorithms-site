Detailed-balance quantum Gibbs sampling

Implementation status: partial

Original Gaussian filtered-jump access and a restricted finite weak reset channel have executed small checks. The general noncommuting correction, uniform Gibbs-accuracy/mixing guarantees, rotation synthesis, physical resources and mechanized proof remain planned. The existing Gibbs examples retain their separate loader and heralded-filter scope.

Motivation and intuition¶

A Gibbs sampler drives a system toward the mixed state ρβ=e−βH/Tr⁡(e−βH)\rho_\beta=e^{-\beta H}/\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H})ρβ​=e−βH/Tr(e−βH).

Frequency filters weight energy-changing proposals, while the quantum generator’s detailed balance fixes the thermal stationary state. A fresh environment records the proposed transition and is then discarded/reset; this turns a unitary system–environment circuit into a channel on the system. Stationarity and the number of rounds needed to approach it are separate questions. Our finite example makes both the transition construction and its residual Gibbs error visible.

Fundamental method and complexity¶

The 2025 Efficient quantum thermal simulation construction uses Gaussian operator-Fourier filters and a corrective coherent term to obtain a synthetic Lindbladian satisfying exact KMS detailed balance. Under the paper’s normalized jump-access condition ∥∑a(Aa)2∥≤1\|\sum_a(A^a)^2\|\leq1∥∑a​(Aa)2∥≤1, evolution for time t≥1t\geq1t≥1 uses signed and controlled Hamiltonian simulation with total time O~(βt)\widetilde O(\beta t)O(βt); preparation still requires the problem’s mixing time Chen et al., 2025.

Exact stationarity of the mathematical generator and accuracy of a finite digital circuit are separate statements. Discretizing filters, truncating local patches, synthesizing rotations and simulating the channel consume their own error budget. Unique stationarity does not establish rapid convergence. The high-temperature results specify regimes in which mixing is provably efficient Rouzé et al., 2026Rouzé et al., 2026.

Important finite-jump variant¶

The finite-jump KMS family provides a second implementation choice. Its LCU-access and filter assumptions must be recorded alongside any simpler jump inventory; fewer jump labels alone are not a physical-resource comparison Ding et al., 2025.

Planned local-circuit comparison¶

Hahn and colleagues provide another implementation route: truncate the quasilocal KMS generator to overlapping Hamiltonian patches, sample short-time local channels, and compile their dilations into local circuits Hahn et al., 2026. Their high-temperature results assume local finite-range lattice Hamiltonians and the stated jump-generator conditions. Approximating the full Gibbs state and estimating a local observable have different truncation-radius requirements; a fixed small patch does not establish global accuracy at every system size.

This comparison remains planned. A local implementation must retain the coherent correction, all dilation/discard/reset operations, randomized-channel sampling and readout. Its ledger must also include patch construction, circuit compilation and any variational-fit error, then allocate truncation, digital-evolution, synthesis, sampling and physical faults separately. The paper’s local compilation and numerical results have not been reproduced here, and no physical improvement over the frozen surface-code baseline is claimed.

Executed finite zero-correction channel¶

The first endpoint uses H=ZH=ZH=Z and normalized Pauli proposals X/2X/\sqrt2X/2​ and Z/2Z/\sqrt2Z/2​. More generally, the source accepts diagonal I/Z Pauli Hamiltonians and Pauli proposals. Each filtered block has a fixed computational-basis permutation multiplied by diagonal coefficients. Therefore the decay operator is diagonal, commutes with HHH, and has exactly zero spectral coherent correction. Noncommuting inputs are rejected by this endpoint; a dense correction matrix is never appended as a gate.

The circuit applies the actual Gaussian access UUU, rotates a fresh weak flag by 2arcsin⁡δηγl2\arcsin\sqrt{\delta\eta\gamma_l}2arcsinδηγl​​ under its frequency register, and applies the full U†U^\daggerU† only when the flag is zero. The mesh normalization η\etaη is retained, with δηmax⁡lγl≤1\delta\eta\max_l\gamma_l\leq1δηmaxl​γl​≤1. Every proposal, frequency and flag outcome contributes to the reduced channel. Environmental reset discards the labels; there is no accepted-only branch or algorithm success retry. The controlled inverse reverses the actual Gaussian PREPARE, signed product-formula evolution, Pauli SELECT and negative DFT, preserving their phases and charging every added primitive control Chen et al., 2025.

The independent reference retains all non-jump Kraus operators ⟨e∣U†I−δηΓU∣0⟩\langle e|U^\dagger\sqrt{I-\delta\eta\Gamma}U|0\rangle⟨e∣U†I−δηΓ​U∣0⟩ and all jump operators δηγeBe\sqrt{\delta\eta\gamma_e}B_eδηγe​​Be​. Nonzero environment labels in the non-jump branch contribute second-order channel terms. Keeping only the zero label would change the implemented channel.

End-to-end resources and fault tolerance¶

The logical workload exposes system size nnn, inverse temperature β\betaβ, proposal count JJJ, clock size M=2mM=2^mM=2m, weak step δ\deltaδ, number of rounds KKK and output. It uses n+m+log⁡2J+1n+m+\log_2J+1n+m+log2​J+1 wires. Every complete round includes the actual access, frequency rotation, primitive conditional inverse and explicit environmental resets. Known input initialization and final measurement are counted separately. Explicit Gaussian loading and weak multiplexing use O(M)O(M)O(M) source rotations; the current Gray-Walsh angle preprocessing uses O(M2)O(M^2)O(M2) classical work. This is the ordinary prototype circuit, rather than the paper’s optimized near-linear Lindbladian simulator.

The emitted readout circuits use eight rounds, with every gate and reset materialized. For a Z mean of that finite circuit output, additive sampling error 0.1 and failure probability 0.05 give 738 independent complete preparations by Hoeffding. The logical projection multiplies every full preparation, reset and readout by that count. No draws or hardware readouts were performed. This sampling contract is separate from stationary mesh bias and the recorded 64-round state errors; it is not a Gibbs-accuracy certificate.
