Detailed-balance quantum Gibbs sampling

Implementation status: partial

Original Gaussian filtered-jump access, a restricted finite weak reset channel, finite weighted-decay block access and a one-spin correction block with one heralded known-input step have executed small checks. Separately accepted staged checks compose a fixed weak channel with two heralded correction steps on one spin. The general noncommuting correction, uniform Gibbs-accuracy/mixing guarantees, rotation synthesis, physical resources and mechanized proof remain planned. The existing Gibbs examples retain their separate loader and heralded-filter scope.

Motivation and intuition¶

A Gibbs sampler drives a system toward the mixed state ρβ=e−βH/Tr⁡(e−βH)\rho_\beta=e^{-\beta H}/\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H})ρβ​=e−βH/Tr(e−βH).

Frequency filters weight energy-changing proposals, while the quantum generator’s detailed balance fixes the thermal stationary state. A fresh environment records the proposed transition and is then discarded/reset; this turns a unitary system–environment circuit into a channel on the system. Stationarity and the number of rounds needed to approach it are separate questions. Our finite example makes both the transition construction and its residual Gibbs error visible.

Fundamental method and complexity¶

The 2025 Efficient quantum thermal simulation construction uses Gaussian operator-Fourier filters and a corrective coherent term to obtain a synthetic Lindbladian satisfying exact KMS detailed balance. Under the paper’s normalized jump-access condition ∥∑a(Aa)2∥≤1\|\sum_a(A^a)^2\|\leq1∥∑a​(Aa)2∥≤1, evolution for time t≥1t\geq1t≥1 uses signed and controlled Hamiltonian simulation with total time O~(βt)\widetilde O(\beta t)O(βt); preparation still requires the problem’s mixing time Chen et al., 2025.

Exact stationarity of the mathematical generator and accuracy of a finite digital circuit are separate statements. Discretizing filters, truncating local patches, synthesizing rotations and simulating the channel consume their own error budget. Unique stationarity does not establish rapid convergence. The high-temperature results specify regimes in which mixing is provably efficient Rouzé et al., 2026Rouzé et al., 2026.

Important finite-jump variant¶

The finite-jump KMS family provides a second implementation choice. Its LCU-access and filter assumptions must be recorded alongside any simpler jump inventory; fewer jump labels alone are not a physical-resource comparison Ding et al., 2025.

Planned local-circuit comparison¶

Hahn and colleagues provide another implementation route: truncate the quasilocal KMS generator to overlapping Hamiltonian patches, sample short-time local channels, and compile their dilations into local circuits Hahn et al., 2026. Their high-temperature results assume local finite-range lattice Hamiltonians and the stated jump-generator conditions. Approximating the full Gibbs state and estimating a local observable have different truncation-radius requirements; a fixed small patch does not establish global accuracy at every system size.

This comparison remains planned. A local implementation must retain the coherent correction, all dilation/discard/reset operations, randomized-channel sampling and readout. Its ledger must also include patch construction, circuit compilation and any variational-fit error, then allocate truncation, digital-evolution, synthesis, sampling and physical faults separately. The paper’s local compilation and numerical results have not been reproduced here, and no physical improvement over the frozen surface-code baseline is claimed.
