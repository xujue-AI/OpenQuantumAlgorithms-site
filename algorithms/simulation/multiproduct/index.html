Multiproduct Hamiltonian simulation

Motivation and intuition¶

Product formulas approximate evolution by alternating easy term exponentials. Multiproduct formulas combine different step counts so that leading errors cancel. A coherent combination uses an actual coefficient register and controlled circuits; a classical combination estimates observables from separate circuits. Their outputs and statistical costs differ Low et al., 2019Vazquez et al., 2023.

Implementation status

The original primitive circuits, 103 focused tests, 14 small numerical cases, seven notebook code cells and standalone replay have executed successfully. A 32-spin circuit bundle was generated without numerical simulation. The checked methods supply conditional states, a discarded-ancilla channel, or classical weighted observables, as specified below. Rotation synthesis, physical resource estimation, literal quantum sampling/retry populations, mechanized proofs and the optimized high-order family remain planned.

The classical variant separately counts its fine/coarse populations and every full circuit. Its ideal and statistical allowances belong to the combined weighted task. Classical simulation, formalization and reproduction¶

Dense verification is capped at three system plus three access/work qubits. The circuit generator allows at most 61 system qubits, 4,096 coarse steps and 200,000 complete primitive operations. Scalar counting allocates no Hamiltonian, state, circuit or size-dependent table. The executed 32-spin generation produced eight exports with 26,096 total instructions; its complete OAA measured trial has 5,427 instructions on 35 logical wires. This checks materialization and source counts, with no dense simulation or inherited accuracy claim. Download its source bundle.

No Lean/Rocq theorem or source-to-proof bridge has been reproduced. The symbolic coefficient, amplification and commutator derivations remain mathematical exposition, distinct from finite numerical circuit checks. All exposition, code and circuit figures are original; we use pinned Qiskit 2.6.2 (Apache-2.0), NumPy 2.5.4 and SciPy 1.18.1 in the locked Python 3.12.14 environment.

The standalone generator, original module, locked environment, MIT license and two-spin measured LCU QASM are downloadable. The small bundle includes the complete study and both SVGs; the separate large bundle contains generation-only circuits and records.
