Amplifying a known thermal preparation

A thermal filter can fail postselection even when its accepted state is accurate. The noncommuting thermal-filter circuit supplies an actual preparation unitary, its inverse and a recognizable success pattern. These ingredients let us amplify a known prepared input without changing the accepted thermal state. The experiment below checks that preservation, counts the extra access and reflections, and shows why a higher one-attempt success rate need not give a cheaper workload.

Implementation status

Known-input amplitude amplification is implemented with explicit full-register and label-register reflections. The one-qubit Bell-plus-filter example is checked for rounds 0–4, including negative global signs and full coherent source/primitive equivalence. Complete measured trials include initialization, label readout, reference reset and separate X/Z measurements. Degree and temperature bias remain unchanged. Adaptive/fixed-point amplification, certified synthesis, fault-tolerant physical estimates and a Lean proof remain planned.

Task, representation and sizes¶

Use the same noncommuting model and units as the thermal-filter page:

H/E0=0.6Z+0.4X,E0=1,kB=1.H/E_0=0.6Z+0.4X,\qquad E_0=1,\qquad k_B=1.H/E0​=0.6Z+0.4X,E0​=1,kB​=1.
(1)

The default has β=1.25/E0\beta=1.25/E_0β=1.25/E0​, Taylor degree K=6K=6K=6 and one system qubit. A second case uses β=2/E0\beta=2/E_0β=2/E0​ to expose overrotation. The requested state is ρβ=e−βH/Tr⁡(e−βH)\rho_\beta=e^{-\beta H}/\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H})ρβ​=e−βH/Tr(e−βH); the measured task estimates its energy from separate X and Z means.

(2)

normalization s=∑j∣bj∣s=\sum_j|b_j|s=∑j​∣bj​∣, m=⌈log⁡2LK⌉m=\lceil\log_2L_K\rceilm=⌈log2​LK​⌉ label qubits and N=2n+mN=2n+mN=2n+m total wires. Zero padding is used in the label register. The default aggregate has three Pauli terms, so n=1n=1n=1, m=2m=2m=2 and N=4N=4N=4.

The actual source circuit AAA starts from ∣0N⟩|0_N\rangle∣0N​⟩, prepares the Bell purification ∣Φ2n⟩|\Phi_{2^n}\rangle∣Φ2n​⟩ with H and CX gates, and applies explicit PREPARE/SELECT/inverse-PREPARE access. Its success projector is

Πf=IS,R⊗∣0m⟩⟨0m∣.\Pi_f=I_{S,R}\otimes|0_m\rangle\langle0_m|.Πf​=IS,R​⊗∣0m​⟩⟨0m​∣.
(3)

The accepted purification has direction (TK⊗IR)∣Φ2n⟩(T_K\otimes I_R)|\Phi_{2^n}\rangle(TK​⊗IR​)∣Φ2n​⟩ and reduced system state

ρK=TKTK†Tr⁡(TK†TK).\rho_K=\frac{T_KT_K^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}(T_K^\dagger T_K)}.ρK​=Tr(TK†​TK​)TK​TK†​​.
(4)

All-zero input preparation is repeatable. No classical eigenvectors or dense exponential are loaded into a quantum gate. The classical matrix answers are small independent references. Pauli coefficient aggregation remains a potentially exponential preprocessing step.

Intuition: reflect about the prepared state through its preparation¶

Write the output of AAA as

A∣0N⟩=p ∣g⟩+1−p ∣b⟩,A|0_N\rangle=\sqrt p\,|g\rangle+\sqrt{1-p}\,|b\rangle,A∣0N​⟩=p​∣g⟩+1−p​∣b⟩,
(5)

where ∣g⟩|g\rangle∣g⟩ is the normalized successful purification and ∣b⟩|b\rangle∣b⟩ lies outside the zero-label subspace. The two reflections are different:

Sf=I−2Πf,S0=I−2∣0N⟩⟨0N∣.S_f=I-2\Pi_f,\qquad S_0=I-2|0_N\rangle\langle0_N|.Sf​=I−2Πf​,S0​=I−2∣0N​⟩⟨0N​∣.
(6)

SfS_fSf​ touches only the labels. S0S_0S0​ touches every input wire, including the system and reference. Applying AS0A†A S_0 A^\daggerAS0​A† reflects about the actual prepared state. Combining this reflection with SfS_fSf​ rotates the two-dimensional plane spanned by ∣g⟩|g\rangle∣g⟩ and ∣b⟩|b\rangle∣b⟩ Brassard et al., 2002.

Both reflections use X gates to turn their zero pattern into an all-one pattern, followed by an H–MCX–H multi-controlled Z and X gates to undo the conversion. An empty success register gives Sf=−IS_f=-ISf​=−I, recorded as a global phase. There is no dense reflection gate.

Starting with AAA, each chronological round is Sf,A†,S0,AS_f,A^\dagger,S_0,ASf​,A†,S0​,A, plus the required global π\piπ phase:

Q=−AS0A†Sf,Ar=QrA.Q=-A S_0 A^\dagger S_f,\qquad A_r=Q^r A.Q=−AS0​A†Sf​,Ar​=QrA.
(7)

Let θ=arcsin⁡p\theta=\arcsin\sqrt pθ=arcsinp​. The known-input identity is

ΠfAr∣0N⟩=sin⁡[(2r+1)θ]p ΠfA∣0N⟩.\Pi_f A_r|0_N\rangle= \frac{\sin[(2r+1)\theta]}{\sqrt p}\,\Pi_f A|0_N\rangle.Πf​Ar​∣0N​⟩=p​sin[(2r+1)θ]​Πf​A∣0N​⟩.

It requires a unitary preparation AAA, but does not require its accepted filter block to be proportional to a unitary. The entire accepted vector receives one scalar. A negative scalar gives a global sign; it leaves the reduced thermal density matrix unchanged. Tests first check that signed raw vector identity, then check phase-invariant purification distance and reduced-state distance.

The system and reference stay coherent through all rounds. Label measurement and unconditioned reference reset occur only after amplification. The inverse A†A^\daggerA† acts on that complete unitary preparation; it does not invert a measured or discarded thermal state.

Why the thermal preparation needs this reflection¶

Oblivious amplification leaves an unknown system input outside its initial reflection and uses an ancilla-only projector on both sides. For a nonunitary filter, that construction generally reshapes its unequal singular values and changes the thermal eigenweights Berry et al., 2014Berry et al., 2015.

A bounded contrast replaces the full S0S_0S0​ here by the labels-only SfS_fSf​. For the default one-round case, the resulting reduced state is about 0.8133 in trace distance from the original polynomial state. The correct known-input construction preserves it to numerical precision. These are different algorithmic contracts: the known Bell input makes the full-input reflection available, without a reflection about an unknown state or quantum cloning.

Implemented choices on this page are unamplified preparation (r=0r=0r=0), fixed integer known-input amplification and the explicitly labeled ancilla-only contrast. Fixed-point/phase-adjusted schedules, efficient estimation of unknown success, scalable thermal-access constructions and physical comparisons remain planned.

Analysis: queries, reflections and size-dependent cost¶

In the ideal small-ppp oracle model, a suitable round count uses O(1/p)O(1/\sqrt p)O(1/p​) complete preparation/inverse calls instead of O(1/p)O(1/p)O(1/p) independent attempts. That statement counts access; it does not remove the preparation, inverse, reflection, coefficient-processing or readout costs.

For rrr rounds the actual circuit contains:

  • r+1r+1r+1 forward calls to the entire Bell-plus-filter AAA, and rrr calls to A†A^\daggerA†;

  • rrr full-input reflections on N=2n+mN=2n+mN=2n+m wires and rrr success reflections on mmm labels;

  • one final label readout, unconditioned reference reset and system measurement per complete attempt.

Each full source call includes the Bell gates and one PREPARE/SELECT/inverse-PREPARE access. Its inverse is an actual inverse circuit. The source preparation and access are counted on all 2r+12r+12r+1 calls; initialization and final readout are charged once per complete attempt. There are no intermediate resets or early-abort savings.

The full reflection can be substantially more expensive than the label reflection. The generated size family uses Hn=∑i(0.6Zi+0.4Xi)H_n=\sum_i(0.6Z_i+0.4X_i)Hn​=∑i​(0.6Zi​+0.4Xi​) at the same β\betaβ and degree. It constructs actual Pauli access and lowers both reflections, before claiming any larger-system state validation:

The one-qubit accepted-shot budget is not extrapolated to larger nnn. The family energy coefficient sum grows as nnn; fixed absolute energy precision requires a new measurement/error allocation, as well as the new preparation probability. Complete energy trials and separated errors¶

The exported X and Z trials reset all four wires, run the amplified unitary, measure the two label bits, reset the reference without conditioning, perform the requested basis change and measure the system. Only zero-label outcomes are accepted; every rejected trial runs the full schedule and is charged. A separate coherent file is supplied for circuit inspection.

Energy estimation uses independent accepted X and Z means,

E^/E0=0.6Z^+0.4X^.\widehat E/E_0=0.6\widehat Z+0.4\widehat X.E/E0​=0.6Z+0.4X.
(9)

A single Z-basis bitstring is not an energy sample for this noncommuting sum. The numerical workload proposal retains the thermal page’s 0.03 statistical energy-error allocation, 0.005 statistical-failure allowance and a distinct 0.005 acceptance-failure allowance across the accepted-shot slots. It multiplies each actual measurement-group circuit by its retry count. Sampling is unexecuted; ideal statevector probabilities and numerical retry projections are not hardware confidence guarantees.

For MMM requested measurement slots, one-trial acceptance prp_rpr​ and retry cap RRR, the ledger distinguishes three complete-trial counts:

E[Nuncapped]=Mpr,E[Nstopped]=M∑k=0R−1(1−pr)k=M[1−(1−pr)R]pr,Nfull cap=MR.\mathbb E[N_{\rm uncapped}]=\frac{M}{p_r},\qquad \mathbb E[N_{\rm stopped}]=M\sum_{k=0}^{R-1}(1-p_r)^k =\frac{M[1-(1-p_r)^R]}{p_r},\qquad N_{\rm full\ cap}=MR.E[Nuncapped​]=pr​M​,E[Nstopped​]=Mk=0∑R−1​(1−pr​)k=pr​M[1−(1−pr​)R]​,Nfull cap​=MR.
(10)

The stopped expectation includes slots that exhaust their cap, and assumes every requested slot is attempted. It is neither conditional on all slots succeeding nor the cost of aborting the whole workload at its first exhausted slot. The named *_uncapped and *_stopped_at_cap fields retain that distinction; legacy expected_complete_trials and expected_complete_attempts fields explicitly identify their M/prM/p_rM/pr​ values as uncapped. Full-cap instruction counts and the proposed physical-fault allocation continue to use MRMRMR.

Verification, exports and reproducibility¶

Tests compare signed accepted vectors with the known-input identity, phase-invariant purifications and reduced states with the original polynomial, and Gibbs states/means with independent matrix-Taylor and normalized-exponential references. Cases include Y terms, a two-qubit Hamiltonian, overrotation and β=0\beta=0β=0 with no label qubits. Complete X/Y/Z trial tests verify reset and measurement semantics through joint density-matrix probabilities.

For the small coherent circuits, source and primitive full-unitary matrices are compared with global phase retained. Lowering explicitly sets qubits_initially_zero=False, so reusable controls cannot silently depend on clean input wires. These checks are numerical evidence for the stated cases; no Lean proof or formal compiler certificate is supplied. Formalization remains planned.

The pinned OpenQASM exporter omits an overall circuit phase. The measured exports preserve their probabilities but are not phase-preserving coherently controlled oracle specifications. The recorded phase and source modules are required for controlled reuse or explicit phase restoration. In-memory signed-vector and full-unitary checks keep that phase.
