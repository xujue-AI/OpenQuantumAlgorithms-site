Quantum signal processing (QSP) turns repeated access to a normalized Hamiltonian into a polynomial of that Hamiltonian. The [Hamiltonian dynamics task](../hamiltonian-simulation.md) fixes the three-spin open chain, $t=1$, input $|000\rangle$ and observable $Z_0$. This method page keeps its algorithm, numerical verification and complete logical costs together.

The [retargetable QSP variant](retargetable-qsp.md) generates phases for changed sizes and times, with fixed local couplings and a requested accuracy. It includes an interval coefficient validator and a separate logical loading-error bound. The frozen $\alpha t=4.1$ sequence on this page remains a checked reference case.

The retargetable variant supplies bounded phase generation and logical-source interval contracts. Deterministic evolution, unsupported higher-degree regimes and certified hardware synthesis remain planned.

## Motivation and the fundamental version

A product formula approximates time evolution by short local rotations. QSP instead treats a Hamiltonian eigenvalue as a signal: for $H|v\rangle=E|v\rangle$, let $x=E/\alpha$ and $\alpha=\sum_j|h_j|$. Since $|x|\le1$, the access circuit and a zero-label reflection preserve a two-dimensional subspace with signal matrix

$$B_x=\begin{pmatrix}x&\sqrt{1-x^2}\\\sqrt{1-x^2}&-x\end{pmatrix}.$$

The qubitization walk $(2\Pi-I)B$ rotates this subspace through eigenphases $\pm\arccos x$. Phase rotations interleaved with access calls interfere so that the accepted component becomes a chosen bounded polynomial in $x$, applied to every eigencomponent without computing eigenvectors {cite:p}`sim_qubitization_2019,sim_qsvt_2019`.

Here $\Pi=|0\rangle\langle0|_{\rm label}\otimes I_{\rm system}$ and

$$D(\phi)=e^{i\phi(2\Pi-I)},\qquad
V=D(\phi_0)B D(\phi_1)\cdots B D(\phi_d).$$

The scalar implementation multiplies matrices in this written order. Circuit gates are applied chronologically in reverse phase order, beginning with $\phi_d$. This convention matters when importing phases. A flag in $|+\rangle$ selects opposite phase signs, then H and flag-zero acceptance extract the real part. The implemented signed phase is $e^{i\phi(2\Pi-I)Z_f}$: an $R_Z(2\phi)$ on the flag and a zero-label-controlled $R_Z(-4\phi)$ supply both contributions. Ignoring a branch phase changes the polynomial.

A degree-$d$ real polynomial needs $d$ complete block calls and $d+1$ projector phases. Parity restricts a single real transform: even cosine and odd sine require separate sequences. For the ideal block-access model, Hamiltonian-simulation degree can scale as $O(\alpha|t|+\log(1/\epsilon)/\log\log(1/\epsilon))$ in the small-error regime {cite:p}`sim_qsp_2017,sim_qubitization_2019`. This is a query bound with specified access; our direct gate construction must also pay coefficient loading, SELECT, controls, uncomputation, synthesis and measured repetitions. Frozen numerical phases below cover one $\alpha t$ and do not implement a general optimal-degree phase compiler.

## Planned generalized QSP variant

Generalized QSP uses general ancilla SU(2) rotations to construct a complex
polynomial $P(W)$ of a unitary signal $W$, with $|P(z)|\leq1$ on the complex
unit circle {cite:p}`ham_gqsp_2024`. Its signal convention removes the fixed
polynomial-parity constraint and can change the cosine/sine combination
used for Hamiltonian simulation. Completing a complementary polynomial and
constructing the rotations are part of the algorithm.

The existing Hermitian $B_x$ sequence, fitted phases and accepted block near
$U/4$ retain their present evidence and retry contract. A GQSP companion is
**planned**: it needs its own unitary signal/access circuit, convention check,
rotation generator, independent small polynomial and evolution checks,
acceptance analysis and full synthesis/preparation/readout costs.
Qualtran's inspected `HamiltonianSimulationByGQSP` is a prospective
implementation source {cite:p}`ham_qualtran_snapshot`; its compositional
model has not been adopted or reproduced here.

## Explicit block access and cosine filtering

The five Hamiltonian terms have $\alpha=\sum_j|c_j|=4.1$. Explicit PREPARE and signed Pauli SELECT give the Hermitian involution

$$\mathrm{PREPARE}|0\rangle=\sum_j\sqrt{|h_j|/\alpha}\,|j\rangle,\qquad
\mathrm{SELECT}=\sum_j|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes\operatorname{sgn}(h_j)P_j.$$

The loader is a tree of controlled RY gates. SELECT applies indexed Pauli gates and coefficient signs, and unused binary labels apply identity. For real coefficients each selected operation squares to identity. The complete access is

$$B=\mathrm{PREPARE}^\dagger\mathrm{SELECT}\,\mathrm{PREPARE},\qquad\langle0|B|0\rangle=H/\alpha.$$

Tests check the actual block. A reflection about the zero-label subspace creates the qubitization walk and its invariant-subspace identity is checked {cite:p}`sim_qubitization_2019`.

The QSP module documents its scalar matrix convention and phase ordering. Fourteen calls to $B$ and fifteen frozen phases produce a degree-14 real polynomial approximating $\cos(4.1x)/2$ on $[-1,1]$. A flag coherently combines the $+\phi$ and $-\phi$ branches to extract the real part, sharing block calls. The phase JSON records a deterministic SciPy fit, independent dense-grid residual, Chebyshev-coefficient discrepancy and analytic polynomial-tail bound. Tests compare the **actual circuit block** to $\cos(H)/2$, separately from the scalar phase check {cite:p}`sim_qsp_2017,sim_qsvt_2019`.

## Complex QSP dynamics with explicit postselection

The next circuit completes the cosine/sine combination for the fixed dynamics task. Freeze an even degree-14 phase sequence for $C(x)\approx\cos(4.1x)/2$ and an odd degree-15 sequence for $S(x)\approx\sin(4.1x)/2$. A uniform selector and a relative $-i$ phase on its sine branch give the zero-selector block

$$K(x)=\frac{C(x)-iS(x)}{2}\approx\frac{e^{-i4.1x}}{4}.$$

This is the QSP/LCU normalization, not a deterministic unitary on the system {cite:p}`sim_qsp_2017,sim_qsvt_2019,sim_qsp_lcu_unification_2021`. The generator shares fourteen ordinary $B$ calls between the branches, adds one actual selector-controlled PREPARE/SELECT/inverse-PREPARE call, and implements every selector-conditioned projector phase. It does not append a matrix exponential gate. All three label bits, the real-part flag and the selector must be zero to accept; all relative branch phases are included in the circuit and in decomposition checks.

## Size, access cost and classical comparison

For the open Pauli chain, $L=2n-1$ nonzero terms and $m=\lceil\log_2L\rceil$ label wires. Cosine uses $n+m+1$ wires; complex evolution uses $n+m+2$. The coefficient loader has at most $2^m-1$ controlled rotations per PREPARE and is inverted on every access. Direct SELECT scans all $L$ labels and every nonidentity letter, rather than assuming a constant-cost oracle. For this local chain it visits $3n-2$ Pauli letters; an arbitrary dense Pauli list can visit $nL$. Multi-controlled gates then expand under the pinned compiler. These are logical source structures, not physical resource estimates.

The executed sweep uses

$$J_n=\frac{4.1}{(n-1)+0.7n},\qquad h_n=0.7J_n,\qquad t=1.$$

Thus $\alpha_nt=4.1$ for every $n$ and the same degree-14/15 phases apply. At $n=3$ this is the chapter's $J=1,h=0.7$ model. Increasing $n$ weakens each coupling. This family measures access/compilation growth at fixed total normalization and phase degree; it does not establish fixed-local-coupling equal-accuracy scaling. At fixed $J,h,t$, $\alpha_n=|J|(n-1)+|h|n$ grows with $n$, requiring phases matched to each normalization and eventually a larger degree. The separate bounded study below retargets degrees 14/15 for $n=2,3,4$ at fixed couplings. The [retargetable method](retargetable-qsp.md) generates new degree/phase choices for bounded requests. Its in-page resource study includes actual Clifford+T sequences and reset-aware measured circuits for one and two spins; logical-source interval contracts, numerical synthesis checks and conditional physical projections retain distinct evidence labels.

The sweep checks gate-based conditional states and $Z_0$ against independent matrix exponentials for $n=1,\ldots,5$. Full accepted-block checks are limited to circuits of at most eight total wires ($n\le3$ here). The $n=4,5$ results check the chosen input state, not all inputs or the complete operator. The CSV records actual primitive counts, depth, continuous rotations, preparation/unprepare and ordinary/controlled access calls, readout and conditional retries.

All zero inputs require fresh initialization but no extra data-preparation gates. The QSP exports and sweep counts omit initial reset instructions as well as their physical time; each attempt requires initialization of all $n+m+2$ wires in the complex circuit. The coefficient PREPARE, its inverse, flag gates and final measurements are included. A different input needs an implemented loader and its inverse when the surrounding algorithm requires one. Each accepted observable sample requires fresh complete attempts, so an accepted-sample workload multiplies the full access/reflection/readout cost by its retry cap. Probability and synthesis changes after postselection must be budgeted separately. The sweep uses the numerically checked ideal acceptance, without a hardware or interval-wide certificate.

For its proposed 13,315 accepted $Z_0$ samples, sampling error 0.03 and statistical failure 0.005 are separate from acceptance failure 0.005 across all slots. The numerical $p\approx1/16$ gives 230 complete attempts per slot, hence a cap of 3,062,450 measured attempts. These workloads are unexecuted projections. At $n=3$ the measured per-attempt count is 8,748, before the excluded initial resets, synthesis and physical cost. The earlier 72-attempt cap concerns one accepted trial with 1% acceptance failure; it is a different output contract. This **13,315-sample logical proposal already allocates .01 to sampling plus acceptance failure**; it cannot acquire the later .005 physical allowance without changing the contract. The physical study instead uses 13,811 samples and .004/.001/.005 allocations.

Dense state and operator answers grow exponentially: a state has $2^n$ amplitudes, a Hamiltonian has $4^n$ entries, and a full unitary simulation includes the access ancillas. They are excellent small-instance checks but supply neither a scalable classical benchmark nor evidence of quantum advantage. Phase-fitting and coefficient-processing CPU work, physical initialization/readout time, routing, factories and error correction are excluded from quantum gate counts.

## Verification and formal evidence

Verification proceeds through independent scalar signal matrices, frozen-phase residuals, exact small Hamiltonians, the accepted gate-circuit block, complex conditional states and measured observables. Tests check Y terms, negative coefficients, branch-relative phases and primitive decomposition. The analytic Bessel tail bounds polynomial truncation; floating coefficient reconstruction and a sampled grid remain numerical evidence. Synthesis must supply an actual coherent norm guarantee before it can be combined with postselection-sensitive state and acceptance bounds.

The static resource study additionally checks all measured QASM hashes against the costed circuits, complete readout/retry counts, synthesis allocations and modeled physical union bounds. Its retargeted basis trajectories and analytic phase-Jacobian checks extend the earlier normalized-sweep evidence; they do not convert numerical phase fitting or compiler equivalence into a theorem.

The current [Python package](../../assets/generated/openquantum-code.zip) includes optional phase recomputation with `fit_retargeted_phases`. A real degree-14 even or degree-15 odd polynomial has eight parity coefficients, so each continuation step uses a fixed eight-dimensional subspace of the Jacobian at its starting phases. The fitter retains a maximum step of 0.2, 500 joint response/Jacobian evaluations per step and a 60-second deadline per component; acceptance requires a maximum node residual $\le10^{-8}$. Checks compare the fitted response with the stored sequences and independent signal matrices, including rotations of the fitting coordinates. The resource circuits use the recorded phase sequences above.

No QSP-specific Lean source, locked proof toolchain or correctness artifact is integrated. QSP Lean coverage is **planned**. A future proof of a signal identity or individual gate would have to state its scope separately from full phase synthesis, approximation, retries and physical correctness. The current evidence is executable numerical verification.
