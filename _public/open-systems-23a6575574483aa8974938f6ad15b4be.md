This track has no implemented channel, dilation circuit, notebook result or resource estimate. Classical density-matrix evolution will serve as a small-instance reference after the quantum access construction is implemented.
:::

An open-system instance will specify an initial density operator $\rho(0)$ and either a finite channel or a time-independent Markovian GKSL generator {cite:p}`sim_lindblad_1976,sim_gks_1976`:

$$
\mathcal L(\rho)=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_k\left(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger-
\tfrac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}\right),\qquad \rho(t)=e^{t\mathcal L}\rho(0).
$$

The next implementation must define the Hamiltonian, jump operators, rates, units and time; choose a requested state or observable; and state a trace-distance or channel-error requirement and sampling confidence. If rates are given separately, $L_k=\sqrt{\gamma_k}A_k$. General open-system evolution need not have this semigroup form. A density-matrix exponential alone will not implement this quantum task.

Circuit evidence will include an actual channel or dilation, environment ancillas, preparation and uncomputation where applicable, reset/discard operations, and the measurement schedule. Comparisons will preserve the same generator, input, time, observable and error metric. Approximation error, statistical error and physical faults will have separate budgets. The first bounded example and its method remain to be selected and implemented.
