## Motivation and intuition

Many quantum tasks encode the answer in an eigenphase: an energy in Hamiltonian evolution, an order in modular arithmetic, or a probability in amplitude amplification. A phase is not directly visible in a computational-basis measurement. Phase estimation uses controlled evolution and interference to turn it into a classical binary record {cite:p}`prim_qpe_1998`.

The quantum Fourier transform (QFT) combines the phases carried by a register. Phase estimation first prepares a superposition of different evolution lengths, then applies an inverse QFT so that matching phases add and mismatching phases cancel. QFT produces a quantum state; it does not return every entry of a classical Fourier transform in one measurement. This chapter supplies reusable components for the survey's applications {cite:p}`survey2025`.

## Fundamental algorithm and task contract

For $N=2^m$, our positive-sign QFT convention is

$$
F_N\lvert x\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_{y=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi ixy/N}\lvert y\rangle.
$$

The circuit uses $m$ Hadamards, $m(m-1)/2$ controlled-phase gates and $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ swaps. Wire `q[0]` is the least-significant bit. Swaps are included, so the matrix is the expression above rather than a bit-reversed variant. The inverse reverses the gate order and the phase signs.

QPE accepts an explicit $n$-qubit circuit $U$ and an explicit preparation circuit for an eigenstate

$$
U\lvert\psi\rangle=e^{2\pi i\phi}\lvert\psi\rangle,\qquad 0\leq\phi<1.
$$

It prepares $m$ phase qubits with Hadamards, applies controlled $U^{2^j}$ from phase wire $j$, applies $F_N^\dagger$ and measures those $m$ wires. The decoded integer $y$ estimates $\phi$ by $y/N$. Our generator constructs each power from **$2^j$ complete controlled calls to the supplied $U$**; no power or dense matrix is treated as a free oracle. A supplied global phase becomes a conditional phase and is retained.

For an eigenstate, the independent reference distribution is

$$
P(y)=\frac{1}{N^2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi ik(\phi-y/N)}\right|^2.
$$

A phase exactly on the grid is recovered with ideal probability one. For other phases, the two adjacent grid points are within circular error $1/N$ and together have probability at least $8/\pi^2$. The median of 15 independent scalar estimates has majority-failure bound below $0.005$ at that single-run bound. **Scalar medians require $\phi\in[1/N,1-1/N]$**, so the good interval does not cross the phase branch cut. A circular median is not implemented. If the input is a superposition of eigenstates, the output is the corresponding phase mixture; a good phase-resolution bound does not establish overlap with the desired eigenstate.

## Important variants

Phase estimation is a family of measurement and inference procedures. The right choice depends on the requested output, the prepared state, available controlled evolution, coherent lifetime and classical processing. Our executed scope includes exact/truncated QFT, textbook QPE, [retained-target feedback QPE](qft-qpe/iterative.md), [fresh-preparation Hadamard inference](qft-qpe/hadamard.md), [bounded circular-grid Bayesian inference](qft-qpe/bayesian.md) and [bounded-bias robust inference](qft-qpe/robust.md). The remaining additional methods below are **literature-backed implementation plans**. Their papers and external software do not validate our circuits or physical resources. ### Shared circuit and resource contract

For an eigenphase $U|\psi\rangle=e^{2\pi i\phi}|\psi\rangle$, a Hadamard experiment with controlled $U^k$ and a phase offset $\beta$ has

$$
\Pr(0\mid k,\beta)=\tfrac12\left[1+\operatorname{Re}\!\left(e^{-i\beta}\langle\psi|U^k|\psi\rangle\right)\right].
$$

Offsets $0$ and $\pi/2$ reveal cosine and sine information. For a mixed spectral input the signal is a weighted sum of eigenphases, rather than one unknown scalar phase. Retaining the target through a measurement sequence and freshly preparing it for every experiment have different joint distributions. The [retained-target energy confidence protocol](energy-estimation.md#small-circuit-and-retained-target-confidence) cannot be transferred to a fresh-preparation sampler without a new argument.

For experiments at Hamiltonian times $t_j$, with $N_j$ shots, record both

$$
T_{\max}=\max_j|t_j|,\qquad
T_{\mathrm{evol}}=\sum_jN_j|t_j|,
$$

and charge every preparation, actual controlled evolution, synthesis allocation, measurement, reset/feedback and classical fit. $T_{\max}$ measures the longest evolution demand; $T_{\mathrm{evol}}$ measures accumulated evolution work. Neither equals physical wall time without compiling the gates and accounting for code cycles, factories, repeated initialization and scheduling. A lower coherent depth can require more shots. Compare variants at the same requested output, accuracy, success, initial-state promise and frozen physical assumptions.

## End-to-end resource analysis

Let $C_U^{(c)}(n)$ denote the compiled cost of one controlled supplied $U$, and $C_{\rm prep}(n)$ its state preparation cost. Before synthesis, conventional QPE requires

$$
G(n,m)=C_{\rm prep}(n)+(2^m-1)C_U^{(c)}(n)+O(m^2),\qquad Q(n,m)=n+m.
$$

With $R$ independent repeated runs, preparation and the entire measured circuit are charged $R$ times; there are $Rm$ phase-readout bits. For phase resolution $\epsilon_\phi=2^{-m}$, the repeated-access term is $O(C_U^{(c)}(n)/\epsilon_\phi)$. Classical median decoding and compilation are excluded from physical runtime and are unmeasured. An application must provide its actual controlled evolution and eigenstate overlap rather than reuse this toy cost.

The [logical scaling CSV](../assets/generated/qft-qpe/scaling.csv) uses an active $n$-wire product of phase gates and prepares $\lvert1\rangle^{\otimes n}$. Each wire contributes angle $2\pi\phi/n$, so the total eigenphase stays $0.3$. Every target wire is used. This supplies explicit $n$ scaling for a specified access model, with $C_U^{(c)}(n)=O(n)$; it is not a model for arbitrary Hamiltonians. Counts include measured preparation and the full decomposed QPE circuit.

## Verification and formalization

The dedicated QFT/QPE suite has 104 cases covering exact transform matrices for one through six wires, inverses, all tested truncation cutoffs, grid and off-grid phases, bit order, coherent global phases, phase mixtures, actual feedback/reset behavior, primitive lowering and rejected opaque/dynamic access. The reference Fourier matrix and Dirichlet distribution are independent mathematical constructions. Additional independent review checked conjugated multi-qubit eigenstates and entangled phase mixtures. These numerical checks cover the tested instances; they are not a theorem for every generator input.

No Lean formalization is integrated or claimed. [SQIR/VOQC](https://github.com/inQWIRE/SQIR) is a relevant external Rocq/Coq project; linking it does not prove this Qiskit generator, its feedback instrument or its physical compilation. Any future formal badge needs a pinned successful proof build and a stated connection to the exact implementation.

The inspected [Lean-QuantumAlg QPE endpoint](https://github.com/QudeLeap/Lean-QuantumAlg/blob/971dc8be8a153a116b6d7cd748f984d545861b00/QuantumAlg/Algorithms/QPE/Endpoint.lean) supplies an additional external reference. Its `QuantumPhaseEstimation.main` contract covers controlled-unitary access, actual phase-register preparation/readout, circular precision and restoration of a borrowed eigenstate. Controlled $U$ and the eigenstate are supplied: the budget charges $2^t-1$ oracle calls, rather than compiling their implementation or eigenstate preparation. Its [QFT endpoint](https://github.com/QudeLeap/Lean-QuantumAlg/blob/971dc8be8a153a116b6d7cd748f984d545861b00/QuantumAlg/Primitives/QFT/Endpoint.lean) uses an independent Fourier operator and native gate budgets. These are inspected source contracts; no kernel build, Python-to-Lean correspondence, synthesized-rotation certificate or physical compilation proof has run here.
