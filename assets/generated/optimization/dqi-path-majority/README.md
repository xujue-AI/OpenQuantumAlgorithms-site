# Path-local majority decoder: accepted saved component

This family exposes one accepted 12-wire local-decoder check and the separate
generation-only 31-wire k=3 DQI source program. It supplies no full-program,
degree-two preparation, optimization-output, synthesis, physical-resource or
formal-proof validation. The original C5 and degree-one evidence remain separate.

## What is retained

The seven files in `generated/` and two in `validation/` are byte-identical
accepted artifacts. Their original manifests/ledgers retain their pending or
source-prediction flags; `accepted-components.json` supplies the later parent
acceptance and finite scope without rewriting raw records. `results-summary.json`
selects recorded values. All 4096 final real/imaginary pairs remain in
`validation/validation.json`. Input and reference vectors were not separately
saved: their fixed signed recipe and numerical source are pinned. This public
projection computes or reconstructs none of those vectors or diagnostics.

Six original source/script/test files are retained both in repository source
locations and under `code/` for direct static download. These `code/` files are
evidence snapshots. This family does not supply a newly executed portable
scientific package or a replacement environment. Source headers describe the
pre-execution freeze and remain byte-identical; the later accepted run is
identified by separate acceptance metadata. Private controllers and their
75 boundary checks, grants, process identities, operational logs and raw caller
records are excluded.

## Accepted finite scope

The component has four syndrome wires, five arbitrary clause-target wires
and three initially clean scratch wires. A full phase-exact CCX lowering
implements 17 compute plus 17 uncompute CCX blocks and 15 external CX gates:
525 primitives (H 68, CX 219, T 136, T-dagger 102). The target XOR is checked
without assuming zero targets; syndrome and clean scratch are preserved.
The global graph promise remains weight at most two despite distance ten.
The off-promise triple-error example returns its weight-two complement and
changes terminal parity. This is not an every-neighbor recovery guarantee.

Parent acceptance confirms 259 passing checks (184 scientific pure checks
plus 75 private control checks), one 4096-amplitude forward evolution and
525 numerical unitary applications. The numerical validator checks 512
arbitrary-target/clean-scratch mappings, and the separate pure scientific table
checks another 512: 1024 whole-job local table iterations. The 121 global
weight-at-most-two words and 8192 classical assignments are separate pure scopes.

The recorded maximum signed-complex entry error is 1.1529425372364673e-15,
auxiliary leakage probability 1.6312041995757796e-30 and preserved-syndrome
probability discrepancy 1.8457457784393227e-15 at absolute tolerance 2e-10,
zero relative tolerance. There is no global-phase fit, renormalization,
postselection, sampling or dense circuit operator. This is finite floating
evidence, not a reproduced formal theorem or a hardware experiment.

## Source accounting and limits

The generated full program has 31 quantum wires, 3114 unitary primitives,
31 resets and 13 readouts. Its 3165 source records include seven barriers;
3158 operational events exclude those barriers. The full source includes the
actual degree-two counter/rotation preparation, signs, syndrome construction,
local decoder, uncomputation, assignment Hadamards and readout. The full
program was not evolved; its prepared state and optimization distribution
remain unvalidated. RY synthesis, scheduling, generic success/repetitions,
physical costs and formal proof remain unavailable for this increment.

Fixed-five-edge local decoding has linear emitted gate count and shares
three clean scratch wires. Dense incidence-mask storage is O(mn) bits;
bounded-word preprocessing at n <= 49 is distinct from producer bit/byte work.
The independent path-parity comparator has an exact linear classical optimum.
This family demonstrates a decoder mechanism and no optimization advantage.
