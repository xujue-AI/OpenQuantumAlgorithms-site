Roadmap

The project grows by completing reproducible examples within a stable chapter structure. The survey Dalzell et al., 2025 guides coverage; implementation order also reflects which algorithms share reusable circuit and estimation components.

Release sequence¶

The current release has bounded implementations across the listed algorithm families: Grover, exact/approximate QFT, textbook/feedback QPE, coherent/adaptive amplitude estimation, reversible arithmetic and bounded factoring, structured HHL/QSVT and Neumann LCU inverses, Hamiltonian dynamics and amplification, channel/Lindblad simulation, thermal preparation, explicit quantum walks and exact/finite-shot QAOA/VQE with digitized annealing. Their analysis and verification are integrated into each algorithm article; significant methods have child pages.

Computed physical reports distinguish static arbitrary-rotation mean-fit costs from a bounded serialized model with actual Clifford+T sequences and reset, controller and factory accounting. Both retain explicit conditional assumptions. Certified hardware realization, general application access, scalable thermal methods and rigorous co-design comparisons remain under development. The frozen Grover baseline stays fixed. A pinned Lean artifact checks a finite classical C4 reference; it does not verify the Python implementation, quantum circuits or physical estimates.

These stages are implementation milestones. They do not imply that later families are less important or that a quantum advantage is established.

Coverage beyond the survey¶

The survey guides the initial coverage; it does not limit later algorithms. The current literature expansion supplies planned method contracts alongside the executed examples:

TrackAdded coverageNext bounded implementation
Hamiltonian dynamicsBounded Suzuki, qDRIFT and fixed two-term coherent/classical multiproduct circuits checkedOptimized high-order multiproduct, interaction-picture and matched physical studies
Phase estimationHadamard-test, Bayesian, corrected robust, statistical energy and window/filter methodsShared controlled-power likelihood experiments with separate coherence and shot accounting
FactoringArithmetic/recovery improvements, Regev-style alternatives, external resource models and Coq/Lean developmentsCompare bounded order recovery on the same circuit-generated samples
Decoded quantum interferometryFixed three-variable coherent decoder and public companion checked Jordan et al., 2025Structured larger decoder families and Hamiltonian HDQI; synthesis and physical accounting
Detailed-balance Gibbs samplingSynthetic filtered-jump thermalization Chen et al., 2025Finite diagonal zero-C channel checked; next: general correction, uniform accuracy and mixing
Reusable bath qubitBounded one-spin gate and finite-channel components checked Ding et al., 2026Continuum/total accuracy, growing system size and full physical accounting

These method families include checked increments and planned extensions; the linked pages state their exact evidence. The finite detailed-balance channel remains partial, with no uniform Gibbs-accuracy or physical gain claim. Thermal convergence retains the papers’ temperature, mixing, access and gap conditions. Factoring proofs remain external until pinned reproduction and a connection to our generators are recorded. A fault-tolerant comparison keeps input, output, accuracy, success and physical assumptions fixed; it cannot inherit another paper’s advantage ratio.

Chapter completion criteria¶

A chapter is complete when it has:

  1. A precise task, access model, input instance, accuracy target, and success criterion.

  2. A small circuit generated from the same implementation used for larger sizes.

  3. A downloadable notebook and a parameterized code entry point with a pinned environment.

  4. A check against a classical result or another independent reference.

  5. Logical-resource accounting that includes the oracle or state preparation.

  6. A physical-resource estimate with its code, hardware assumptions, compilation choices, and error budget.

  7. Paper citations, software attribution, licenses, and a record of the tested version.

  8. Motivation and intuition, fundamental complexity or scoped empirical performance, important variants, and size-dependent space/time accounting with other parameters explicit.

An incomplete item remains marked planned, partial, or unavailable. A successful small simulation does not establish scalable performance.

Reusable components¶

Early work should create components that later chapters can reuse:

  • Circuit generators with explicit register conventions and ancilla cleanup.

  • State preparation, controlled operations, and reversible arithmetic.

  • A common logical-resource record: qubits, operations, depth, precision, and repetitions.

  • A physical-estimation interface that records the model rather than hiding its assumptions.

  • A consistent method for saving figures, results, environment versions, and references.

Baseline before co-design¶

The initial comparison point is a named surface-code model. Later work may change synthesis, arithmetic, scheduling, factory choices, error correction, or the application representation. Each comparison will preserve the task’s input, output, accuracy, and success requirements, then report both the resource change and the extra assumptions.

See resources for the baseline boundary and verification for evidence levels.
