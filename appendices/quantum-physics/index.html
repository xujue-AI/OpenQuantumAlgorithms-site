Quantum physics for algorithm implementations

Quantum algorithms use quantum physics to transform information and extract a specified result. This appendix introduces the finite-dimensional language used throughout the handbook. Read it alongside the mathematics primer, then follow the quantities you actually need to reproduce an algorithm.

States and observables¶

A pure state is a normalized complex vector ∣ψ⟩|\psi\rangle∣ψ⟩. For one qubit,

∣ψ⟩=α∣0⟩+β∣1⟩,∣α∣2+∣β∣2=1.|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle, \qquad |\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1.∣ψ⟩=α∣0⟩+β∣1⟩,∣α∣2+∣β∣2=1.
(1)

Relative phases affect interference. Multiplying the whole vector by the same phase leaves its physical state unchanged. A state describes the system; an observable describes a measurable quantity. Observables are Hermitian operators, such as the Pauli ZZZ operator or the Hamiltonian HHH, which represents energy.

A density matrix also describes mixtures and subsystems entangled with an environment:

ρ=∑jpj∣ψj⟩⟨ψj∣,pj≥0,∑jpj=1.\rho=\sum_j p_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|, \qquad p_j\geq0,\quad \sum_jp_j=1.ρ=j∑​pj​∣ψj​⟩⟨ψj​∣,pj​≥0,j∑​pj​=1.
(2)

It is positive semidefinite and has trace one. Pure states have ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣; different ensembles can describe the same mixed state. Ignoring part of a joint system gives its reduced density matrix by a partial trace. IBM’s density-matrix lesson develops this broader description.

For example, ∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​ and the mixture ρ=I/2\rho=I/2ρ=I/2 both give equal computational-basis probabilities. They differ when measured in the XXX basis: the pure state always gives the +1 outcome, whereas the mixture gives either outcome equally often. Matching one measurement distribution therefore does not establish that two states agree.

What a measurement returns¶

For an observable A=∑aaPaA=\sum_a aP_aA=∑a​aPa​, a projective measurement returns eigenvalue aaa with Born-rule probability

p(a)=Tr⁡(Paρ),⟨A⟩=Tr⁡(Aρ).p(a)=\operatorname{Tr}(P_a\rho), \qquad \langle A\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(A\rho).p(a)=Tr(Pa​ρ),⟨A⟩=Tr(Aρ).
(3)

The projectors PaP_aPa​ include any degeneracy. Measuring a pure state in the computational basis gives p(x)=∣⟨x∣ψ⟩∣2p(x)=|\langle x|\psi\rangle|^2p(x)=∣⟨x∣ψ⟩∣2. One shot produces an outcome; estimating an expectation generally requires repeated preparations and measurements.

For the physical connection between eigenvectors and measurement, watch Barton Zwiebach’s MIT 8.04 video, “Completeness of eigenvectors and measurement postulate”. Its official lesson page includes the video and transcript.

Dynamics and open systems¶

Closed-system evolution is unitary:

ρ(t)=U(t)ρ(0)U(t)†,U(t)=e−iHt/ℏ\rho(t)=U(t)\rho(0)U(t)^\dagger,\qquad U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar}ρ(t)=U(t)ρ(0)U(t)†,U(t)=e−iHt/ℏ
(4)

for a time-independent Hamiltonian. Circuit gates approximate or compose such transformations; the simulation chapters usually set ℏ=1\hbar=1ℏ=1.

Noise, discarded ancillas, and measurements averaged over their outcomes require quantum channels. A completely positive, trace-preserving map has a Kraus representation

E(ρ)=∑jKjρKj†,∑jKj†Kj=I.\mathcal E(\rho)=\sum_jK_j\rho K_j^\dagger, \qquad \sum_jK_j^\dagger K_j=I.E(ρ)=j∑​Kj​ρKj†​,j∑​Kj†​Kj​=I.
(5)

Complete positivity ensures valid states even when the input is entangled with another system. Trace preservation retains total probability. A selected measurement branch can instead decrease the trace; that trace records the branch’s success probability. IBM’s channel representations explain the channel viewpoint and its implementations.

Thermal targets and accuracy¶

For a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian, the Gibbs state at inverse temperature β=1/(kBT)\beta=1/(k_BT)β=1/(kB​T) is

ρβ=e−βHZ(β),Z(β)=Tr⁡(e−βH).\rho_\beta=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z(\beta)}, \qquad Z(\beta)=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H}).ρβ​=Z(β)e−βH​,Z(β)=Tr(e−βH).
(6)

The partition function normalizes the state; exponentiating a Hamiltonian alone does not produce a normalized density matrix. Energy eigenstates receive weights proportional to e−βEje^{-\beta E_j}e−βEj​. See David Tong’s statistical-physics notes on the canonical ensemble.

Choose an output metric before estimating resources. A search task needs a correct answer with stated probability. An energy task needs observable accuracy and sampling confidence. A state-preparation task may use trace distance D(ρ,σ)=12∥ρ−σ∥1D(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1D(ρ,σ)=21​∥ρ−σ∥1​, which bounds changes in measurement-event probabilities. Fidelity measures similarity; specify whether it is squared or unsquared, as IBM’s fidelity lesson emphasizes. The resource baseline accounts separately for algorithmic approximation, sampling requirements, and physical faults.
