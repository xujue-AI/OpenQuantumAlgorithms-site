Mathematics for quantum algorithms

The mathematical task is to connect a problem specification to a circuit and a checkable output. This primer collects notation used by the algorithm chapters. It complements the quantum-physics primer; no background in formal proof software is required.

Vectors, inner products, and registers¶

A ket ∣v⟩|v\rangle∣v⟩ is a complex column vector; its bra ⟨v∣\langle v|⟨v∣ is the conjugate transpose. The inner product and Euclidean norm are

⟨v∣w⟩=∑jvj‾wj,∥v∥2=⟨v∣v⟩.\langle v|w\rangle=\sum_j\overline{v_j}w_j, \qquad \|v\|_2=\sqrt{\langle v|v\rangle}.⟨v∣w⟩=j∑​vj​​wj​,∥v∥2​=⟨v∣v⟩​.
(1)

Orthogonal vectors have zero inner product. An orthonormal basis gives coordinates without changing lengths. Normalized state vectors have unit norm, while input data vectors may require a separate normalization factor. IBM’s inner-products lesson develops these conventions.

Two registers combine by a tensor product: ∣v⟩⊗∣w⟩|v\rangle\otimes|w\rangle∣v⟩⊗∣w⟩. Dimensions multiply, so nnn qubits have 2n2^n2n basis states. A joint vector need not factor into individual-register vectors; this is the mathematical possibility behind entanglement. Register order and bit order must match the generator, simulator, and classical comparison. A statevector containing every amplitude therefore has very different storage requirements from a circuit description.

Operators and spectra¶

Matrices act linearly on vectors. The adjoint A†A^\daggerA† conjugates and transposes; a unitary satisfies U†U=IU^\dagger U=IU†U=I and preserves inner products. A Hermitian matrix satisfies H†=HH^\dagger=HH†=H and has real eigenvalues with an orthonormal eigenbasis:

H∣uj⟩=λj∣uj⟩,H=∑jλj∣uj⟩⟨uj∣.H|u_j\rangle=\lambda_j|u_j\rangle,\qquad H=\sum_j\lambda_j|u_j\rangle\langle u_j|.H∣uj​⟩=λj​∣uj​⟩,H=j∑​λj​∣uj​⟩⟨uj​∣.
(2)

Phase estimation targets the phase in U∣uj⟩=e2πiϕj∣uj⟩U|u_j\rangle=e^{2\pi i\phi_j}|u_j\rangleU∣uj​⟩=e2πiϕj​∣uj​⟩. The prepared state’s overlap with the relevant eigenvector affects the chance of obtaining that phase.

Watch Gilbert Strang’s MIT 18.06 video, “Lecture 21: Eigenvalues and eigenvectors” for the underlying eigenvector equation. The official lesson includes a video and transcript; quantum phase estimation applies this idea to unitary operators.

Norms and approximations¶

A norm makes “close” quantitative. The operator norm

∥A∥=sup⁡∥v∥2=1∥Av∥2\|A\|=\sup_{\|v\|_2=1}\|Av\|_2∥A∥=∥v∥2​=1sup​∥Av∥2​
(3)

measures the largest amplification of a vector. The trace norm ∥A∥1\|A\|_1∥A∥1​ is the sum of singular values, and the Frobenius norm ∥A∥F=Tr⁡(A†A)\|A\|_F=\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(A^\dagger A)}∥A∥F​=Tr(A†A)​ measures all entries together. These norms give different error guarantees.

For Hermitian HHH, define f(H)f(H)f(H) by applying fff to its eigenvalues. If a polynomial ppp approximates fff uniformly on an interval containing the spectrum, then

sup⁡x∈[a,b]∣p(x)−f(x)∣≤ϵ⟹∥p(H)−f(H)∥≤ϵ.\sup_{x\in[a,b]}|p(x)-f(x)|\leq\epsilon \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|p(H)-f(H)\|\leq\epsilon.x∈[a,b]sup​∣p(x)−f(x)∣≤ϵ⟹∥p(H)−f(H)∥≤ϵ.
(4)

This motivates polynomial methods for evolution and inversion. QSP/QSVT circuits also impose encoding, boundedness, and parity conditions; an arbitrary polynomial fit alone is insufficient. PennyLane’s QSVT introduction explains this connection.

Linear-system algorithms must additionally track conditioning. For an invertible matrix, the spectral condition number is κ=σmax⁡/σmin⁡\kappa=\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}κ=σmax​/σmin​. Small singular values can make inversion sensitive and expensive. Producing a state proportional to A−1bA^{-1}bA−1b also leaves its normalization and requested readout to be accounted for. It does not directly return a classical list of every solution component.

Probability and confidence¶

For independent preparations with success probability ppp, RRR trials all fail with probability (1−p)R(1-p)^R(1−p)R. Repetition costs belong in the algorithm estimate.

(5)

Thus M≥ln⁡(2/δ)/(2ϵ2)M\geq\ln(2/\delta)/(2\epsilon^2)M≥ln(2/δ)/(2ϵ2) suffices for confidence at least 1−δ1-\delta1−δ. John Duchi’s Stanford notes state the independence and boundedness assumptions. Sampling confidence controls random variation; it does not remove a biased circuit or an inaccurate model. Specify those errors separately when comparing quantum and classical outputs.
